## 1. The whole picture

```
   people and agents talk on a transport (Matrix first): rooms, messages, presence, files, invites
  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ room !abc:matrix.example        human app   agent host   executor   (all are "actors")  │
  │   chat ··· typing ··· files        │            │           │                            │
  │                                    └── HAN entries (signed) ┘                            │
  └──────────────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                             ▼
                    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
                    │  HAN sequencer for this room             │  orders entries, signs receipts,
                    │  Log · Policy · Identity                 │  runs Policy (pure functions)
                    └──────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                             │ receipts (hash-chained)
                                             ▼
                    verifiers: any member's client or server replays Policy and checks every receipt
```

A **transport** carries the conversation: rooms, people, bots, messages, presence, invites and files. HAN doesn't replace it. HAN adds a **sequencer** per room (a HAN *space*). The sequencer orders every authority-bearing fact into a signed, hash-chained log, and runs **Policy**, a set of pure functions, so any participant can re-check every decision.

HAN logs only what agent safety needs:

| What | Entry kinds |
|---|---|
| **Ask for work** | `task`, `task.update`, `task.cancel` |
| **Touch the world** | `effect.propose`, `effect.approve`, `effect.claim`, `effect.outcome`, `effect.resolve`, `effect.cancel` |
| **Govern** (who may do what) | `space.create`, `member.set`, `policy.set`, `key.add`, `key.revoke`, `redact`, `checkpoint`, `space.move` |
| **Work live** (who may watch, read or steer whose session) | `session.open`, `session.input`, `session.close`; `agent.unguard` (a guard ends its agent's guarded choice) |
| **Annotate the record** | `message`: a remark that belongs *in* the authoritative log, e.g. a reason for an approval. Chat stays on the transport. |

**<!--s:kinds_n-->22<!--/s--> kinds, <!--s:calls_core_w-->four<!--/s--> sequencer calls, one log per space.** A live session's *stream* (output, context, status) is not in the log: it flows on the transport (T-07). The log holds only its authority: that it exists, who may watch, read and steer it, and every steer. Everything else is the transport's job (§8).

### 1.1 A worked example: a human hires an agent

Ada (`@ada:a.example`) wants a code fix from an agent, `@fixer:b.example`, operated by Ana (`@ana:b.example`). The agent's changes are pushed by an executor, `@git:c.example`, operated by `@ops:c.example`. They share a Matrix room bound to HAN space `r1:a.example`, sequenced by a.example's HAN sequencer. Amounts are in `USD-cent`. This example is also the vector `conformance/vectors/*-worked-example-1.1.json`.

| # | Who | Entry | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | b.example, c.example | `profile:@fixer:b.example` holds `space.create {profile: {actorKind: "agent", operator: @ana}}`, then Ana's `member.set {actor: @ana, role: owner}`. `profile:@git:c.example` is set up the same way. | both non-human actors exist, and their human operators have confirmed them (§4.3) |
| 1 | Ada | `space.create {policy: {join: "invite", allowAgents: "with-operator", approvals: [{action: "git.push", approvers: ["owner"]}], budget: {unit: "USD-cent", amount: 500}, launchWindow: 30, …}}` | the space exists; Ada is owner. On Matrix, the room carries a state event naming the space and its sequencer (§8.2). |
| 2 | Ada | `member.set {actor: @fixer:b.example, role: member}`, and the same for `@git:c.example` | P-03 checks the confirmed operators. Ada also invites them to the Matrix room (T-04). |
| 3 | Ada | `task {to: [@fixer], budget: {USD-cent, 300}}` + content `{ask: "fix the login bug"}` | 300 of the 500 is reserved for this task (P-05) |
| 4 | fixer | `task.update {state: accepted}`; `effect.propose {executor: @git, action: "git.push", cost: {USD-cent, 50}, task, planDigest}` + content `{plan}` | the proposal waits for approval (P-08) |
| 5 | Ada | `effect.approve {proposal, planDigest}` | phase `open` |
| 6 | git executor | journal `claiming`; send `effect.claim {proposal, attempt: 1, instance: k1, window: 30}` | at the claim's seq, Policy re-checks the executor's and the agent's membership, operators and keys, the approval and the budget (E1–E6). It reserves 50 from the task and pins the 30 s window. |
| 7 | git executor | journal `launching`; check the plan against its digest; deadline check on its own clock; push | X-03/X-04/X-09 |
| 8 | git executor | `effect.outcome {result: succeeded, started: yes, cost: {USD-cent, 0}}` + evidence blob | the 50 goes back to the task; phase `succeeded` |
| 9 | fixer | `task.update {state: done}` + content `{text: "pushed abc123"}` | the task closes; its unspent 300 returns to the space budget |

If the executor crashes between steps 6 and 8, X-06 reports `failed`/`not started` or `unknown`; it never launches again on its own. If Ada bans the agent, or Ana withdraws her confirmation, before step 6, the claim is denied (E2). If Ada shortens `launchWindow` after step 6, the claim keeps its pinned 30 s (P-09). If a.example's sequencer dies, every verifier still holds the verified log, and Ada continues in a fork (§7.4).

## 2. The primitive: entry, content, receipt

Exact shapes: [han.ts](han.ts) `Entry`, `Receipt`, `LogRecord`.

An entry has two parts:
- the **header**, which holds every field Policy needs (`body`) plus routing and signing fields. It is permanent;
- the **content**, which holds human-meaningful payload (text, plans, results, blob lists). It is redactable.

```
Entry       = { v:1, space, kind, author, keyId, body, contentHash, refs, seen, nonce, time, sig }   -- exactly these fields
contentHash = sha256( JCS(content) )             -- "" when no content is sent
id          = sha256( "han-entry-v1\n" ‖ JCS(entry minus sig) )
sig         = Ed25519( key(author,keyId), "han-sig-v1\n" ‖ id )
Receipt     = { space, seq, id, prev, home, homeKey, time, durability, profiles, homeSig }
receiptHash = sha256( "han-receipt-v1\n" ‖ JCS(receipt minus homeSig) )
homeSig     = Ed25519( key(home,homeKey), "han-home-sig-v1\n" ‖ receiptHash )
prev        = receiptHash of seq−1 ("" for seq 1)
LogRecord   = { entry, content, redacted, redactedBy?, receipt }   -- redacted: true ⇒ content is null, redactedBy is the proof
```

Here **home** means the space's current sequencer server. Every `‖` concatenates UTF-8 bytes. `id` and `receiptHash` enter signatures as their 64-character lowercase hex strings.

**`receipt.profiles`** has one key per pinned actor (P-01) and, for each, the seq of that actor's profile space the sequencer used to judge the entry (R-10). Three cases have no profile seq to read, and are fixed here: (a) for the server actor `@:S` the value is `0` (its view is synthetic, §4.3); (b) for the actor whose profile space the entry is **in** (as its author, or as the target of the operator's `member.set`) the value is the head *before* that entry (`seq − 1`; its view there is that space's own state at the entry's position), so a `key.add` that is seq 4 of `profile:@x:S` pins `{"@x:S": 3}`; (c) the `space.create` of a profile space, authored by `@:S`, pins `{"@:S": 0}` and nothing else. Every other entry pins its actors at their profile heads (golden: alice's `space.create` in `s1:a.localhost` pins `{"@alice:a.localhost": 1}` because her profile space holds one record). A verifier replaying a receipt takes the pinned seqs from `profiles` and from nowhere else.

**Encoding.** Frames are I-JSON (RFC 7493): duplicate object keys, invalid Unicode (including lone surrogates) and non-integer number forms (`1.0`, `1e3`, `-0`) are `invalid`. JCS is RFC 8785, including its key ordering by UTF-16 code units (vector `00-jcs-utf16-order`). Every number, including inside opaque Json such as `plan.args` and extension bodies, is an integer in [0, 2^53); a frame holding an integer of 2^53 or more anywhere is `invalid` (G-07 says on which id it is answered). The one exception is the state's `budgetLeft.amount`, which may be negative (P-06). Public keys are raw 32-byte Ed25519 keys and signatures are 64 bytes; both are base64url without padding. Reference: [ref/wire.py](../../ref/wire.py).

**base64url** (keys, `sig`, `homeSig`, `proof`, `accept.sig`, blob `data`, the hello `challenge`) is valid only if it uses the alphabet `A-Z a-z 0-9 - _`, has no `=`, has a length that is not 1 mod 4, and is **canonical**: decoding and re-encoding gives the same string, so the unused low bits of the last character are 0. A receiver MUST answer anything else `invalid` wherever base64url appears. A signature that decodes but does not verify is `invalid` too; a non-canonical one is `invalid` before any verification (wire vector `P28b`).

**Grammar and limits** (also in han.ts). Every pattern below is ASCII and must match the **whole** string (no trailing newline, no non-ASCII digits or letters). Exceeding any size limit is `invalid`. The header limit binds first, so the list caps below are upper bounds, not guarantees.

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| ActorId | `@` + name `[a-z0-9._-]{0,64}` + `:` + ServerName; the empty name is the server actor. On Matrix, a HAN actor *is* the Matrix user of the same ID. |
| ServerName | lowercase DNS name: labels `[a-z0-9]([a-z0-9-]{0,61}[a-z0-9])?`, dot-separated |
| SpaceId | `[a-z0-9]{1,26}` + `:` + ServerName (the space's **first sequencer**; the name `profile` is reserved), or `profile:` + ActorId |
| EntryId, Hex | `[0-9a-f]{64}` |
| nonce | `[a-z2-7]{26}` (128 bits, base32) |
| KeyId | `[A-Za-z0-9_-]{1,64}`; single-use per actor (§4.3) |
| Time | `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ`: UTC, a real proleptic Gregorian date, seconds 00–59, no fraction. A sequencer writes `receipt.time` by **rounding its clock down** to the second. Times compare as instants, and a time is **expired** when it is at or before the time it is compared with (`expiresAt` ≤ `ctx.time`). |
| action | `[a-z0-9][a-z0-9._-]{0,127}`; `*` only in approval rules |

| extension kind | `<prefix>.<name>`: prefix `[a-z][a-z0-9-]{0,31}` and not a core prefix; name `[a-z0-9][a-z0-9._-]{0,63}` |
| maxDelegationDepth | 0–16 |
| launchWindow | 15–3600 seconds |
| publicKey | base64url, 1–128 chars |

**R-01 Idempotent append.** If an entry's `id` is already in the space, `append` MUST return the stored receipt *before any other check*, including signature, key validity, schema, policy and limits, and change nothing. The id stays in the log forever, so this holds for the life of the space.
**R-02 One chain.** A space's receipts form one chain (`seq` 1, 2, … with `prev` linking). The **home at seq k** is the sequencer named in the space id, changed by every `space.move` ordered before `k`. Two kinds of portable misbehaviour proof exist:
- *equivocation*: two valid receipts with the same `(space, seq)` but different `receiptHash`, signed by the same server;
- *wrong home*: a valid receipt at seq `k` signed by a server that wasn't the home at `k`.

A space may move back to a former home. Any verifier knows the current home from the chain.
**R-03 Durable before receipt.** A sequencer MUST NOT return a receipt before the record is durable at the stated `durability`: `"local"` survives process crash, `"replicated"` survives the loss of any one machine. It MUST NOT state a level it has not achieved.
**R-03a Receipt time.** Within a space, `receipt.time` MUST NOT decrease, including across a move. A sequencer MUST derive receipt times from a clock that doesn't jump: a steady, slewed clock anchored at startup, never a wall clock that can be stepped. If its clock is behind the last receipt time (for example after a move), it MUST answer `unavailable` until it catches up (§4.1 step 7, so only for an entry Policy allows). Every party's clock MUST keep its rate within 1% of true time. Verifiers check non-decrease, and flag a receipt time more than `maxClockSkewSeconds` (60, han.ts `Limits`) ahead of their own clock as a fault (`future-time`). That one tolerance applies everywhere a party compares a time with its own clock.
**R-04 Immutability and redaction.** Headers and receipts never change and are kept for the life of the space (no compaction, so every `after` stays readable and every verifier can replay from seq 1). "Append-only" refers to them: a content store MAY be rewritten to honour `redact`. After a crash a torn last line of storage is dropped: nothing in it was acknowledged, and the sender's R-06 retry appends it again and gets a fresh receipt. `redact` (P-12) deletes the *content* wherever honoured and sets the record's `redacted` flag. A redacted record MUST carry `redactedBy`: the signed `redact` entry and its receipt, which verifiers check (R-14), so a sequencer can't silently erase content.
**R-05 Same-space references.** `refs` and every `EntryId` inside a `body` MUST name an entry already in the same space; otherwise the entry is `invalid`. Cross-space links are content only (`Link` in han.ts) and carry no authority.

## 3. The sequencer interface

Every HAN binding offers these <!--s:calls_core_w-->four<!--/s--> operations. A **generic binding** (§8.1) carries them over one WebSocket at `wss://<server>/han`, with the byte-level rules of G-07…G-11 (§8.3). The **Matrix binding** (§8.2) carries them as room events.

| Call | Args → result | Retry |
|---|---|---|
| `hello` | `{who, keyId, proof, version, knownHeads?}` → `{limits, durability, time, knownHeads}`. `proof = Ed25519(key, "han-hello-v1\n" ‖ challenge ‖ "\n" ‖ dialed)`: the exact bytes, what `dialed` is, the challenge and the error codes are G-08. `limits` has the <!--s:limits_w-->ten<!--/s--> members of han.ts `Limits`. `knownHeads` (≤ 256) are answered only for spaces the asker named and may read (R-15). | new connection |
| `append` | `{entry, content?}` → `Receipt` | **always safe with the same entry** (R-01) |
| `read` | `{space, after, follow?, limit?, knownHeads?}` → stream of `ReadItem`: **entries only.** First `{type:"heads"}` if `knownHeads` (≤ 256 receipts) was sent (R-15; present, possibly empty, whenever `knownHeads` was sent); then `{type:"record"}` in seq order, only for seqs the reader may read (P-02). `limit` (1–1000, default 1000) bounds the backlog of records. Without `follow` the stream then ends; with `follow` it continues with new records. If the backlog exceeds `limit`, a `follow` sends `limit` records, then the live records from the head at registration on; the skipped records are a visible gap in `seq`, and the client MUST re-read the gap (`read` from its last contiguous seq). A reader who may read no seq gets `not_found`, the same as for a missing space. `read` with `follow` of a space the server does not have, or that the reader may not read, is answered at once (`not_found`, or `moved`, §4.1 step 2) and opens no stream: a server MUST NOT hold the request open. A follower that expects the space (an executor, a verifier, a host) MUST retry until it gets a stream or `denied`, waiting 1 s after the first failure and doubling up to 30 s. `read` with `follow` delivers records only; signals are delivered on `subscribe` (G-09). The remaining argument, item-order and boundary rules are G-09. | safe |
| `state` | `{space, at? \| cursor? \| effect? \| agent?}` → `{state, next?}` (han.ts `StateReply`): **one page of the space's `StateView`**, the JCS of `SpaceState` minus `entries`, an optional member that is absent being omitted. It needs read rights at the head (P-02): a reader who may read some seq but not the head gets `denied`, one who may read none `not_found`. `cursor`, `effect` and `agent` exclude each other; `at` combines with `agent` only. See below. | safe |

**`state`: paging, selectors, bounds.** *Paging (H3).* Nothing that grows is repeated. The members `roles`, `roleHistory`, `tasks`, `effects`, `sessions`, `guarded` and, in a profile space, `profile.keys` (han.ts `PagedMap`, **in this order**) are cut. `guarded` is a set, sent as a map whose every value is `true`; the others are maps. Every one is present on every page (as `{}` when it has nothing on that page), a page holds at most `maxStateItems` (1,000, han.ts `Limits`) items of each, and every item is sent on exactly one page. Only the scalar members (`space`, `head`, `home`, `budgetLeft`, the rest of `profile`) and `policy` are repeated. *Fill order.* The maps are taken in the order above, each adding its next items one at a time while it has added fewer than `maxStateItems` on this page and the reply, as the frame `{"id": <16 digits>, "ok": {"state": …}}`, stays within `maxFrameBytes` less 256 bytes kept for `next`; an item of a map costs the JCS bytes of its key plus those of its value plus 2. The first item that does not fit **closes the page**: no later map adds anything to it. So two conforming servers cut one state into the same pages, and a page is full of the earlier maps before it holds any of a later one. *Cursor.* `next` is `<head>.<o1>.….<oN>`: the head the pages are at (the head of the state read, or `at`), then for each paged map in the order above the number of its items the earlier pages sent, all decimal without leading zeros (`7.2.0.1000.0.0.0.0`, seven offsets). A client treats it as opaque, but a server rejects every other string, a head that is 0 or beyond the current head, and an offset beyond the map's size at that head, as `invalid`. A client that wants the whole state repeats `state {cursor: next}` until `next` is absent and unions the paged maps; the cursor carries its own head, and read rights are judged at that head as for `at`. *Nothing is refused for its size.* Every item is bounded by §2: a task has at most 64 assignees, a task or an effect at most 19 `guardedFor` agents (P-15), an effect at most 55 approvals (P-08) and 64 claims (`MAX_ATTEMPTS`, P-07), a `roleHistory` entry 64 intervals, every key is an ActorId, KeyId or EntryId, and `policy` is within the header limit. With the real `maxFrameBytes` the repeated members and any single item therefore always fit a page. A server asserts this; it never answers `unavailable`, or any code, for a state it cannot cut, because that cannot happen (a test that lowers `maxFrameBytes` with `test.limits`, G-11, keeps its states small enough). *Selectors.* `at` (a Seq in 1…head; anything else, a non-integer included, is `invalid`) gives the state after record `at`, with `head` = `at`; it needs read rights at `at` (P-02, judged on the current state), not at the head, so a former member can read the state as it was while they were in, and a member who joined later can read an earlier state. `cursor` gives the page it names. `effect` (an EntryId) gives `{space, head, home, policy, effects: {<id>: …}}` (han.ts `EffectStateView`), the one effect and nothing that grows, never cut and without `next`: it is what an executor or a host reads (X-03, R-24); a non-string `effect` is `invalid`, an unknown one `not_found`. `agent` (an ActorId; anything else is `invalid`) gives `{space, head, home, policy, guarded}` (han.ts `AgentStateView`): the set `guarded` cut to that actor, `{}` when the actor is not in it, never cut and without `next`. It is a `GuardInput` that `guardStatus` takes as is (R-24), so a host needs nothing else besides the profiles it has pinned and does not page the whole state. With `at`, the facts are those of the state after record `at` (rights judged as for `at` above). **The selectors exclude each other by shape:** `cursor`, `effect` and `agent` are exclusive, and `at` combines with `agent` only, so any other two or more present is `invalid`, judged before any value is looked at and by presence, whatever the values (`null` included). There is no other combination to refuse, because `state` takes no `after`, `follow`, `limit` or `knownHeads`.

**Errors:** `denied` (policy or authentication; never retry the same entry) · `invalid` (malformed, bad signature, bad schema, over a size limit; never retry) · `not_found` (never) · `limit` (rate limit; retry after `retryAfterMs`) · `unavailable` (same request later, after `retryAfterMs` when given) · `moved` (same request at `movedTo`, a ServerName). When more than one applies, the order of §4.1 decides, and it is the only order: `invalid` and `denied` never change, so they win over `limit` and `unavailable`, which are about this moment; the one exception is a check that cannot be made without something that is momentarily missing, the author's profile (§4.1 step 4).

**R-06 Unknown outcome.** If `append` fails with `unavailable` or the connection drops, the outcome is unknown. The sender MUST retry the identical entry until it gets a receipt or a definitive error (`denied`/`invalid`), and MUST NOT express the same intent with a fresh nonce meanwhile.
**R-07 Outbox.** Every actor MUST durably store each signed entry (and content) *before its first send*. After a restart it MUST finish sending every stored, unconfirmed entry under R-06 before appending anything new to that space.
**R-08 No silent downgrade.** An unsupported version MUST fail with `invalid`. A sequencer that can't currently reach the space's `minDurability` MUST fail appends with `unavailable`, except `policy.set` and `space.move`, which are always ordered so a space can't lock itself. A sequencer MUST refuse (`unavailable`) a `space.create` or `policy.set` asking for a `minDurability` it can never provide. Both come after Policy has allowed the entry (§4.1 step 7): a forbidden or forged entry is `denied` or `invalid`, never `unavailable`.

Policy is <!--s:policy_fns_w-->six<!--/s--> functions, but its interface carries no state and has no side effects: everything it hides is *rules*. The executor and agent host are **actors**. Each is built with a `Client` (the <!--s:calls_core_w-->four<!--/s--> calls plus the transport) and fed every record and signal of the spaces it follows, as `ReadItem`s. It answers only through its client. That's the same interface a human app implements.

### 4.1 M1 Log: the sequencer pipeline

On `append(entry, content)` the sequencer runs these steps, **in this order, which is the one order of verdicts: the first step that fails decides the answer and nothing after it is looked at.** Steps 2–10 run in one serial transaction per space.

1. **Dedupe:** if `id` exists, return its receipt (R-01).
2. **Not home?** Routing is by the space id and by what the server holds. It applies to **every** call that names a space (`append` including `space.create`, `read`, `subscribe`, `signal`, `blobGet`, `watch`, `stream`), and it comes before any look at the entry's shape:

   | Situation | Answer |
   |---|---|
   | The server has no record of the space, and the ServerName in the space id is **not its own** (for `profile:@x:S`, `S` is that name) | `moved`, `movedTo` = that ServerName. A `space.create` is `moved` too, never `invalid`, and so is a malformed entry |
   | The server has no record of the space and the id **names this server** | read-type calls (`read`, `subscribe`, `signal`, `blobGet`, `watch`, `stream`): `not_found`. `append` of a `space.create`: the server decides by its own rules whether to host it, and declining is `denied`. `append` of any other kind: Policy's `invalid` (P-16 step 4), which an entry reaches only after steps 3 and 4 |
   | The server moved the space away (`space.move`, R-02), or holds the records but is not the space's home (a mirror, or a move target before its first record, §7.3) | `moved`, `movedTo` = the current home |

   A client MUST retry a `moved` call at `movedTo` (looked up through `/.well-known/han`), and MUST NOT treat `not_found` or `moved` of a follow as final when it expects the space to appear (§3, D5).
3. **Shape:** the header has exactly the `Entry` fields, each in its grammar (§2); the body matches the kind's closed schema (R-09); sizes are within limits (the header limit is on the Entry as sent, `sig` included). **Content** rules: `content` absent and `content: null` are the same thing, and are allowed when `contentHash = ""`, or when every content field of the kind is optional; content present (not null) with `contentHash = ""` is `invalid`; `contentHash ≠ ""` with no content is `invalid` for a new entry (a resend of a stored entry never reaches here: step 1 answers it, without content); a kind whose content is "—" (§5) with any content is `invalid`; present content matches `contentHash` and the kind's content schema and checks (§5: `planDigest = sha256(JCS(plan))`, `plan.action = action`). For a `space.move`, `accept` verifies (§7.3). All of these are `invalid`. `seen` ≤ head and the existence of `refs` (R-05) are not checked here: they belong to Policy (P-16 step 5). R-25 says which of these checks `Policy.decide` repeats and which it never sees.
4. **Profile and signature.** (a) Pin `Identity.profile(author)`. A profile of an actor on another server is read from that server's HAN sequencer as the server actor `@:S'` (it authenticates with the keys in `/.well-known/han`, G-08), where profile spaces are world-readable; the pinned seq is that space's head as read, never lower than one pinned before (R-10), and the read is "within 300 s" by the sequencer's own steady clock. If the author's profile cannot be had now, or the remote server cannot be reached, the entry is `unavailable`: this is the one `unavailable` that comes before Policy, because without the author's keys the entry cannot be judged at all. (b) `Identity.verify(entry, profiles[author])`. The signature is checked against the public key of `keyId` in the pinned view whatever that key's status, so a forged signature under a revoked key is `invalid`, and a genuine one under a revoked key is Policy's `denied` (R-12). A `keyId` that is **absent** from the view has no public key to check against: the entry is `denied` (R-12, "no valid key"), and no signature check is made. A signature that is not canonical base64url (§2) is `invalid`. (c) Only then pin the other actors `relevantActors` names, calling it again with what was fetched until it names nothing new (P-01, R-10), every call with `ctx.time` = the same `now` that step 5 gives Policy and that becomes the receipt time (the clock is read once per append, so an approval that expires between two readings cannot change the list); the receipt pins exactly its last list, and if one on that list cannot be had, the entry is `unavailable`, since Policy cannot decide without it. An actor tried on an earlier call but not on the last list (a candidate after the first valid key) is neither pinned nor needed. A forged entry never gets this far, so it is `invalid` and never retried.
5. **Policy:** `Policy.decide(state, entry, ctx)` with `ctx = {time: now, profiles}`. `now` is the steady clock rounded down (R-03a), read once per append: the `now` of step 4(c) and the receipt's `time`. `denied` or `invalid`. An owner's abandon that Policy would allow at claim time + `A` counts as allowed here and is refused in step 7 (R-16).
6. **Rate limits** and block lists: `limit` with `retryAfterMs`. They come after Policy, so a malformed or forbidden entry is `invalid` or `denied` even from an author over its limit, and only a well-formed, allowed entry is `limit`.
7. **The moment-dependent `unavailable`.** Only an entry that passed steps 1–6 is refused for a reason that depends on the moment, and the reasons are tried in this order: the R-16 early abandon (`retryAfterMs` is the larger of the two remaining waits); a clock that is behind the last receipt (R-03a; `retryAfterMs` is the server's own estimate of the wait, a positive integer whose value is not pinned: the two reference servers differ); a `minDurability` that cannot be reached now (R-08), or, for a `space.create` or `policy.set`, one the server can never provide. A `policy.set` and a `space.move` are never refused for a `minDurability` that cannot be reached now (R-08).
8. Assign `seq`, `prev`, `time`, `durability`, and `profiles: {actor: profileSeq}` (§2 says what an own-profile author, `@:S` and a profile's creation pin). Sign.
9. Make durable at the stated level. Then `Policy.fold`, which never fails for an allowed entry (`ref/fuzz_fold.py`).
10. Return the receipt, and publish the record on the transport (T-01).

**Order of verdicts.** The steps above are the order and there is no other: dedupe (a stored entry gets its receipt), routing (`moved`, `not_found`), shape and sizes (`invalid`), the author's profile and the signature (`unavailable`, `invalid`, `denied` for an unknown `keyId`), Policy (`denied`, `invalid`, P-16), rate limits (`limit`), and last the moment-dependent `unavailable`. So a `denied` or `invalid` entry is never `limit` or `unavailable`, a client never retries a doomed entry, and the only `unavailable` before Policy is a profile that cannot be fetched, where the verdict genuinely cannot be computed. Adjacent pairs are each pinned by a wire scenario (ref/run_wire.py, "verdict order"). A sequencer does not check `Entry.time` (it is informational, never a Policy input; R-03a's tolerance is the verifier's). A sequencer answers a `knownHeads` list (R-15) only with the heads of the listed spaces the asker may read and does not otherwise act on the receipts in it: comparing heads is a verifier's job.

**R-09 Closed shapes.** Unknown fields, wrong types, impossible dates or out-of-range values in a known kind's header, body or content are `invalid`.
**R-10 Profile freshness.** The profile seq a sequencer records for an actor MUST be ≥ every profile seq it has recorded for that actor in this space before. It MUST be fetched within 300 s of the receipt `time`, from the profile's sequencer or from a verifier of that profile space. Verifiers check monotonicity exactly. Freshness is a stated trust assumption (§7.5).
**R-16 Abandon needs elapsed time.** A sequencer MUST NOT order an owner's abandon (P-09) unless its own steady clock shows at least `A` seconds since it ordered the claim. This holds even if receipt times say otherwise, so a clock step can't shorten the wait. Moves are denied while a claim is open (P-11), so the same sequencer ordered both. A steady clock can't measure across a restart or a failover to another machine, so after either the sequencer MUST wait a full `A` seconds of its new steady clock before ordering an abandon of any claim ordered earlier. **Which code** (step 5 treats such an abandon as allowed, step 7 refuses it). An owner's abandon that is refused only because it is early (by this clock, or by the receipt time it would get, P-09) is answered `unavailable` with `retryAfterMs` = the remaining wait, so the owner's R-06 retry delivers it when due. The test is: `Policy.decide` evaluated with `ctx.time` = claim time + `A` would allow the entry, which means the claim is the effect's latest, its phase is `claimed`, and the author and key are right. In any other case time is not the only problem, and the sequencer answers Policy's own verdict now (`denied` or `invalid`): an abandon of a settled or superseded claim, by a non-owner, with the wrong shape, or of an unknown claim is never `unavailable`, so a client doesn't retry it forever. Policy's own verdict on a record ordered too early is `denied` (P-09), which is what a verifier replaying it sees.

### 4.2 M2 Policy: pure functions

**R-11 Determinism.** Policy's <!--s:policy_fns_w-->six<!--/s--> functions depend only on their arguments. `ctx.time` is the receipt time, and `ctx.profiles` are the profile views at the seqs pinned in the receipt. Every conforming implementation MUST give identical results; [ref/policy.py](../../ref/policy.py) is the reference and [conformance/vectors/](../../conformance/vectors/) the tests. Wall clocks, outside reputation and rate limits are *not* inputs. Rate limits and block lists live in Log (step 6) and can only refuse.

**R-25 Validation boundary.** Two layers judge an entry, and a verifier replaying the log (R-14) runs both.
- **Log** (steps 1–4, and the limits of step 6) owns the wire form: the header has exactly the `Entry` fields, each in its grammar (§2), including `nonce`, `time`, `contentHash` and `sig`; `id` is derived as in §2 and drives dedupe (R-01); content is present iff `contentHash ≠ ""`, matches it, and fits the kind's content schema (§5); every size and list limit; the signature against the pinned key (a bad one is `invalid`, a revoked key is Policy's `denied`, R-12); for a `space.move`, the signature in `accept` (§7.3); the canonical base64url form of every key (a `publicKey` of a `key.add` or of a profile's `space.create` must decode to 32 bytes and be canonical, else `invalid` at step 3; Policy checks only its grammar, because vectors stub keys); rate limits and block lists.
- **Policy `decide`** is called with an entry that already passed those. It checks the fields it reads (`v`, `space`, `kind`, `author`, `keyId`, `refs`, `seen`) and the whole `body` against the kind's closed schema (R-09). That covers every ActorId, EntryId, KeyId, Time, Budget and `PolicyDoc` inside the body, and, for an extension kind, a body that is an object of Json holding only integers in [0, 2^53). Everything else it does is P-16.
- `decide` and `fold` **never read** `nonce`, the header `time`, `contentHash`, `sig`, `accept.sig` (the body check needs `accept` to be `{keyId, sig}` with a KeyId and any string), content, or an entry `id`. An implementation MUST NOT reject on them inside `decide`. Header fields outside `Entry` are the Log's to reject; `decide` MAY ignore them (`ref/policy.py` also rejects them, and accepts a vector-only `id`).
- The body shape is checked by both layers (Log step 3, P-16 step 1) and fails as `invalid` in both, so each layer reaches the same verdict alone. `seen ≤ head` and the existence of `refs` are Policy's alone (P-16 step 5): the Log does not check them, so their place in the order of checks is fixed by P-16 and nowhere else.
- `decide` doesn't need the entry's own id. `fold` takes it from `record.receipt.id` (equal to the §2 derivation for every record the Log accepted), and otherwise reads from the receipt only `seq` and `time`.
- **Vectors may stub what the Log owns**, because they are written by hand: ids are labels hashed to 64 hex digits (`00-entry-id` tests the real derivation separately), there is no `sig`, `nonce` is a placeholder, `accept.sig` is any string, `contentHash` is `""` without content, receipts carry only `space`, `seq`, `id` and `time`, and profile views hold dummy keys. A Policy that rejects these is checking the wrong layer. Vectors never stub what Policy owns: no vector holds a body that breaks R-09.

### 4.3 M3 Identity: identities are spaces too

- An actor ID is `@name:server`; on Matrix it is the Matrix user ID. HAN keys are separate from transport credentials. HAN never trusts a transport login as authorship (R-12).
- The actor's **profile space** `profile:@name:server` is sequenced by `server`'s HAN sequencer and is world-readable. Its `space.create` is appended by the server actor `@:server`, with `profile: {actorKind, operator?, key}` naming the first key. **Verifiers of any space MUST also verify, retain and serve the profile spaces of every actor pinned in its receipts**, so profiles outlive their server.
- `key.add` / `key.revoke` may be appended by the actor (signing with any valid key, R-12) or by `@:server` (account recovery); anyone else is `denied`. **KeyIds are single-use**, so a revoked key never becomes valid again: a `key.add` of a keyId already in the profile (even a revoked one), and a `key.revoke` of a key that is not currently valid (unknown or already revoked), are `invalid` (P-16). Revoking an actor's last valid key is allowed. A stolen key can revoke the others, and the server can add keys and so impersonate its users. Both are stated trade-offs (§7.5).
- **Agents and services** MUST name an `operator`, who must be a different, **human** actor: a non-human profile whose `space.create` names none is `invalid` (vector `agents-name-an-operator`); a human's names none. The operator is fixed when the profile space is created and **never changes**: it confirms with `member.set {actor: <operator>, role: "owner"}` in the agent's profile space, withdraws with `role: "none"` (the server may also withdraw), and may confirm again. An actor naming itself as operator is `invalid`. The operator's confirmation and withdrawal are the only `member.set`s a profile space accepts (P-03), so another operator means another actor.
- **Server keys and the two HTTPS documents.** A server's HAN keys are published at `https://<server>/.well-known/han`, fetched over TLS. A receipt is valid only if its `time` lies in its key's `[from, until)`. A key marked `compromised` is trusted only for receipts a verifier recorded before first seeing the mark. Every server MUST keep the key sets it has used for verification; a key that changes for an already-seen window is a fault. These two documents are part of the HAN wire surface, and both are `200 application/json; charset=utf-8` on a `GET` (a verifier MAY cache them for up to 300 s):

  ```
  GET /.well-known/han        {"server": ServerName, "sequencer": URL, "keys": [Key]}      all three members
  GET /.well-known/han-peers  {"peers": [{"server": ServerName, "url": URL}]}              "peers": [] when none
  Key = {"keyId": KeyId, "publicKey": base64url, "from": Time, "until"?: Time, "compromised"?: true}
  ```

  `sequencer` is the complete WebSocket URL of the generic binding, including its path (`wss://a.example/han`, D7). `from` and `until` are Times (§2). `han-peers` lists the other servers this server has verified with, never itself; `url` is the `/.well-known/han` document of that peer (so a verifier can compare the key set this server saw with the peer's own, and with its own retained copies). A server MUST NOT list itself. Absent optional members are omitted, never `null`. Han.ts: `WellKnownHan`, `WellKnownPeers`.
- **Which key signs what (the server actor `@:S`).** The keys of `@:S` in the synthetic ProfileView are exactly the keys of S's `/.well-known/han` whose window `[from, until)` contains the receipt time, each with status `valid` and its listed `keyId`. A server key's KeyId is any string of the KeyId grammar; a verifier MUST NOT assume a particular one (`h1` and `srv1` both occur). The key that signs a receipt (`homeKey`) and the key that signs an entry authored by `@:S` are chosen independently from that set; both are verified from the document, nothing else. A key is "valid" for an `@:S` entry exactly when the entry's receipt time lies in its window.
- **Profile-space bootstrap.** The first record of `profile:@x:S` is a `space.create` authored by `@:S`, signed by one of S's keys, with body `{policy, profile: {actorKind, operator?, key: {keyId, publicKey}}}` and content `{name: "@x:S"}` (the ActorId). Who may ask S to create a profile (registration) is out of scope; the entry is an ordinary entry with exact bytes. Its receipt pins `{"@:S": 0}` (§2). Policy never reads a profile space's `PolicyDoc` (profile spaces are always readable, P-02, and their rules are fixed by P-03 and the table in §6.1), so any valid `PolicyDoc` is acceptable to a verifier; a sequencer SHOULD write exactly this one, so profile spaces look alike everywhere: `visibility: "public"`, `join: "closed"`, `membersMayInvite: false`, `allowAgents: "any"`, `approvals: []`, `separateApprover: false`, `budget: null`, `maxDelegationDepth: 0`, `minDurability: "local"`, `launchWindow: 30`, `extensionsMinRole: "owner"`, `sessions: {watch: "nobody", read: "nobody", steer: "nobody", modes: ["auto"]}`, `servers: {deny: []}`.

- **Adding a device (enrollment).** A new device generates its own key and shows its public key, for example as a QR code. An existing device of the same actor appends `key.add` for it. If no existing device is available, the server appends it (account recovery). Clients MUST then show a visible warning in every space where that actor is a member, because recovery is also how a server could impersonate the actor (§7.5).
- **Finding a profile.** `profile:@name:server` is read with `read` from the HAN sequencer at `server`, whose endpoint is the `sequencer` member of `https://<server>/.well-known/han`, or from any verifier that retains it.

`ProfileView = {actor, seq, actorKind, operator?, operatorConfirmed, keys: {keyId: {publicKey, status}}}` is the fold of the profile space through `seq`. The server actor `@:S` has a synthetic view: `actorKind: "service"`, `seq: 0`, and `S`'s keys valid at the receipt time (above). Inside a profile space, the author's view is that space's own state at the entry's position, and the receipt pins the seq before the entry (§2).

**R-12 Identity is keys.** An entry's author is the actor whose key `keyId` signed it. The key's status in `profiles[author]` must be `valid`; otherwise the entry is `denied` by Policy. A bad signature is `invalid`. Neither the transport sender nor any relaying server determines authorship. Revocation is not retroactive, except that a claim re-checks the keys behind its proposal and approval (P-07 E2, E3).

### 4.4 M4 Verifier

A verifier is any party that checks a space: a member's client, a member's server, or an auditor. `verify(space)` reads the log (`read` with `follow`), checks it, retains it with the profiles and keys it needs, and gossips heads.

**R-14 Verifiers check everything.** For each record, a verifier MUST check:
- the exact header and the content (R-09). A `redacted` record is checked through its `redactedBy` proof instead: a validly signed `redact` entry targeting it, with a later receipt in this space, which the verifier re-checks when it reaches that seq;
- the entry signature against the pinned profile view;
- the receipt: signed by the **home at that seq** (R-02), with a key valid at the receipt time;
- `prev` continuity, R-03a and R-10 monotonicity;
- for a `space.move`, the target's `accept` signature;
- the pins: the keys of `receipt.profiles` are exactly `relevantActors(state, entry, {time: receipt.time, profiles: the pinned views})` (P-01), so a home that pins more (every actor it tried) or less is caught;
- a replay of `Policy.decide`.

On failure it stops trusting the space above the last good seq, tells the room (T-05) with `{type: "fault", proof}`, and keeps the proof. A `proof` is a portable `FaultProof` (han.ts): `equivocation` and `future-time` need only the receipts (the latter with the verifier's `observedAt`); every other kind carries the verified `chain` before the fault, the `profiles` chains it pins, the offending `record` and the `serverKeys` used, so that anyone can replay the check from nothing against server keys they trust. A proof can't be stripped of content, because the content hash check needs it, so a proof of a private space is private. A verifier reports `ok`, `stopped` (it couldn't continue: connection lost, a seq gap because P-02 hid records from it, an unavailable profile chain; never a fault), `faulty` (with the proof) or `moved` (`VerifierStatus`).
**R-15 Head gossip.** In `hello` (both directions) and in `read` (`knownHeads` argument; `{type:"heads"}` item), each side lists the spaces it asks about. The other side answers only for listed spaces the asker may read, as `@:asker` for a server (P-02). A mismatch that forms an equivocation or wrong-home proof is handled as an R-14 failure. Outside `hello` and `read`, verifiers gossip in the space as signals (T-05): heads as `{type: "heads", receipts: Receipt[]}`, faults as `{type: "fault", proof: FaultProof}` (han.ts `GossipSignal`). A receiver compares received heads with its own records, and adopts a fault only if the proof checks against its own server keys; a proof that doesn't check is ignored. Signals carry no authority (T-05), and only readers of the space can send or receive them (G-04). A server verifier MUST exchange heads with at least one other verifier of each space it verifies, at least every 10 minutes.

### 4.5 M5 Executor: the only thing that touches the world

An executor is an actor, usually of kind `service`, that owns part of the world: a checkout, a VM, a mailbox, an account. Each running process (an **instance**) holds one of the actor's keys; `effect.claim.body.instance` is that keyId. Workspaces and snapshots are executor-private, and appear in HAN only as plans and blobs. `stallBound` = 10 s. The executor runs this protocol inside `deliver`:

| Step | Rule |
|---|---|
| **X-01** | Only the instance whose keyId equals `claim.instance` may act on that claim. Its launch journal is durable and private to that instance. |
| **X-02** | Before the first send of a claim, durably journal `claiming(claimId)` with the signed claim and **the reading of its elapsed-time clock at that moment** (R-07). The reading is taken once, before the first attempt to put the claim on the wire, and is never refreshed by a retry: "the first send" is the first attempt, whether or not it connected and whether or not the bytes left (R-06). That clock MUST keep counting through suspend, sleep and VM pause: `CLOCK_BOOTTIME` on Linux, `mach_continuous_time` on macOS, `QueryInterruptTime` (or `GetTickCount64`) on Windows, or the platform's equivalent. An executor MUST NOT run on a platform that can't provide such a clock, MUST log which clock it uses at start-up, and MUST record the clock's name in every `claiming` journal entry beside the reading, so a journal read later shows which clock a reading came from. |
| **X-03** | The claim states the window it expects (`body.window`) and the attempt number (`body.attempt`). Policy accepts the claim only if the window equals the space's `launchWindow` (E5) and the attempt is 1 + the number of earlier claims of the proposal. To build a claim the executor therefore needs `policy.launchWindow` and the proposal's claims so far: it takes them from `state {effect: <proposal>}` (`policy.launchWindow` and `effects[proposal].claims`) or by folding the records it follows (Policy.fold). `W` = `claim.window` is then the only window it uses. A claim built on a stale value is `denied` (E5): the executor re-reads and builds a new claim, it does not guess. Act only after the claim's receipt is in hand. |
| **X-04** | Durably journal `launching(claimId)`. Then, **as the last step before launching**, check that less than `W − stallBound` has passed on the elapsed-time clock since the first send (X-02). If not, durably journal `expired(claimId)`, do not launch, and report `{result:"failed", started:"no"}`. The platform MUST ensure no more than `stallBound` passes between this check and the launch. |
| **X-05** | Report with `effect.outcome`. `started: "no"` only if `launching` was never journaled, or `expired` follows it. |
| **X-06** | **No launch after restart.** On restart, for each `claiming` without `launching`: finish the claim's append (R-06). If it has a receipt, report `{result:"failed", started:"no"}`; if the append ends `denied` or `invalid` (the proposal was cancelled, the executor demoted), there is no claim and nothing to report: journal `refused` and stop. For each `expired`: report the same as for a claim with a receipt and no `launching`. For each `launching` with neither `expired` nor an outcome: look the result up in the outside system if possible, otherwise report `{result:"unknown", started:"unknown"}`. For each result journaled but not yet reported: report it. After a restart the executor never launches; the deadline is not re-armed. |
| **X-07** | An instance key MUST NOT be held by two running processes at once, and MUST NOT be restored on a new machine without its journal. Losing the journal means retiring the key (`key.revoke`), after which the owner abandons its open claims (P-09). A refusal to start (a second process, or a key without its journal) is a process exit with a non-zero status and a message naming X-07 on stderr, so a test harness can tell it from a crash; a lock left by a crashed process MUST NOT block a restart (the executor checks that the holder is alive). |
| **X-09** | Before launching, check that the plan to be run hashes to the proposal's `planDigest`. A plan whose content was redacted can't be run (P-12 prevents this). An executor checks the plan before it journals `claiming`, and again before `launching`. It claims every `open` effect it is the executor of, at most once per head, and lets Policy judge (E3). A `denied` claim is not free: it costs a journal write, a signature and a pass of the sequencer's pipeline. So the executor claims each effect at most once per head: after a refused claim it waits for the head to move before it tries that effect again, and after a failed attempt it also backs off (X-11). When several instances of one actor race, the second claim is `denied` (the phase is `claimed`); the loser journals `refused` and does nothing. |
| **X-10** | If an `effect.outcome` is denied because an owner already abandoned the claim, the executor MUST report what really happened with `effect.resolve`, attaching its journal and outside-system evidence. `evidence` is one BlobRef to a `application/json` document `{"journal": [...], "outside": {...}}`: the instance's journal entries for that claim in order, and a free-form object of what was found in the outside system (`{}` if nothing). |
| **X-11** | After a `failed` / `started: "no"` outcome, an executor SHOULD back off (exponentially, capped: 1 s doubling to 60 s) before claiming the same effect again, so a broken environment doesn't burn attempts. An executor that won't run an effect (an unsupported action, a plan it can't check) SHOULD tell the proposer with a `message` whose `refs` name the proposal and whose `to` is the proposer, once per proposal per process life, and MUST NOT claim it. |
| **X-12** | An executor MUST follow every space it is configured with, and a follow answered `not_found` or `moved` (the space doesn't exist yet) is retried with back-off until it gets a stream or `denied` (§3, D5). The defaults are 1 s after the first failure, doubling to a cap of 30 s, reset once a stream opens; jitter MAY be added. A follow answered `moved` is retried at `movedTo`, whose URL is the `sequencer` of `https://<movedTo>/.well-known/han` (or one configured for that server, for example from `han-peers`); an executor that cannot look it up keeps retrying the URL it has. It reports `cost` only when the action has one; an outcome without `cost` settles the full reservation (P-06). |

**Why no launch follows an abandon.** Let `d` be the drift bound (1%). Define a clock's rate as clock seconds per true second, within `[1−d, 1+d]`. Each side measures only an interval on its own clock, so clock offsets cancel.
1. The executor launches at most `W` executor-seconds after the send. That is at most `W/(1−d)` true seconds.
2. The claim reaches the sequencer no earlier than it was sent. Its `receipt.time` is rounded down, so it is at most 1 s early.
3. An owner may abandon only at sequencer time ≥ `receipt.time + A`, with `A = ⌈W·(1+d)/(1−d)⌉ + 2` (P-09), and only after `A` steady-clock seconds (R-16). That is at least `(A − 1)/(1+d)` true seconds after the send, which exceeds `W/(1−d)`.

So the abandon always comes after the latest possible launch, whichever way each clock drifts. The model checker covers this in its timing and drift scenarios, each with abandon, crashes, arbitrary delivery delay, sub-second arrival with rounded receipts, and stalls on the executor clock:
- *timing*: executor clock offsets, and the owner shortening `launchWindow` mid-claim;
- *drift*: the sequencer's clock 20% fast and the executor's 20% slow (exaggerated; the formula takes the bound as a parameter).

### 4.6 M6 Agent host

The standard does not define how an agent thinks. Agents are ordinary actors that read and append. The host's interface to its agents is `deliver(item)`.

**R-17 Reconstructible inputs.** An agent's entries MUST set `seen` and `refs` so that what drove them is identifiable. Model-provider calls aren't HAN entries; an operator who wants them auditable attaches them as a blob in the content of the `task.update`.
**R-18 Recovery is outbox + reading.** After a crash a host first finishes its outbox (R-07), then re-reads its spaces. It MUST NOT re-propose an intent whose proposal is in its outbox or the log.

**R-24 Hosts honour steer modes.** A host running a live session acts on input from anyone other than the session's actor only once that input is a `session.input` with a receipt (likewise a `task` or an effect that names its agent). Such an instruction is **outside** when its author is neither the agent nor the agent's guard. The host reads `state {agent: <the agent>, at: <the instruction's seq>}`, the agent's facts after the instruction's own record (so a later policy change can't alter how an earlier instruction is treated; one small reply, where paging the whole state would be many), and calls `guardStatus(reply, ctx, agent)` (P-15) with the reply as is (han.ts `GuardInput`: `{policy, guarded}`) and the receipt's pinned profiles as `ctx.profiles` (from its own cache); it builds no state and derives nothing from `forceGuarded` or the sessions itself:
- **Strict turns.** A host MUST run a turn in the **stricter mode** when it is driven by an outside instruction and the agent is `guarded` in the result (it chose guarding, or owners force it); and, **whatever the agent's own guarding**, when it is driven by an instruction whose author is guarded at the instruction's seq (`guardStatus` of the author, read the same way), or by a `task` whose `guardedFor` is not empty. The last two are duties of every agent's host, not only a guarded agent's: they carry a guarded delegator's strictness to its delegates, whose effects also need the delegator's guard while they are assigned its task (P-15).
- **The stricter mode** is the same for every agent, guarded or not, whatever the harness: the turn has **no allow rules**; every call that reaches outside the session's sandbox becomes an `effect.propose`, naming the driving task if there is one, instead of running; and **nothing in the turn is approved locally** (no rule, prompt or tool gate turns such a call into a local run). Who approves the effect is Policy's business (E3), never the local tool gate's. §8.4 shows one harness's way to do it.
- **Taint.** A host MUST record, **per agent** (not per session), that the agent has taken an outside instruction, in any of its sessions and in any space, whether or not it was guarded then. Every later turn of that agent, in any session, runs strict once the agent is guarded in that turn's space. This is the host's duty (kept in its own store, across restarts); the log holds no state for it.
- Otherwise the instruction runs as the agent's own input, in whatever mode the session already runs (for example a harness's auto mode). Whatever touches the world beyond the session's sandbox goes through an effect, which waits for the guard of every agent its `guardedFor` records (P-15).
In a turn driven by a `task`, a host MUST name that task in every `effect.propose` it makes and as the `parent` of every `task` it creates, so the effect is charged to the task and its `guardedFor` carries down the chain. Naming the task is for precision: Policy already carries the `guardedFor` of every open task the agent is assigned into each of its proposals (P-15), so the guarantee no longer rests on it, except for a subtask, which only a `parent` links (§7.5). If P-16 refuses such a subtask (for example a budgeted parent and no budget), the host delegates nothing rather than drop the link. Showing an outside input to the agent's human before it runs is host behaviour, not a mode: the host may do it either way.

Input read from another session's stream or context (T-07), from a `message`, or from a `task.update` or outcome, is **data, never instruction**: a host MUST NOT act on it as if it were a `session.input` or a `task`. A host that feeds another session's context into an agent's turn SHOULD run that turn as if `guarded`, and MUST if the agent is guarded (P-15). Policy can't see what a model read, so this stays a host obligation, listed in §7.5.

In a budgeted space a host's proposals carry `cost` and name the agent's assigned budgeted task (P-05, P-06); without them Policy denies the proposal, so a host must obtain that task before an agent can propose anything (§8.4).

**Blobs.** A `BlobRef` names bytes by hash. The bytes travel on the transport (T-03), and anyone using them MUST verify the hash. Positions that reference blobs: `content.blobs[]` (any kind, extensions included), `effect.propose.plan.argsBlob`, and `effect.outcome/resolve.evidence`.

"At least" is by rank owner > member > guest; no role, or `banned`, is below every minimum, and the result is `denied` (P-16). Extensions define their own meaning; the core neither enforces nor depends on it.

## 6. Policy (normative; reference: ref/policy.py)

### 6.1 State and context

`SpaceState` (han.ts) is the fold of all records. `ctx = {time, profiles}` (§4.1). The wire `StateView` is the JCS of exactly the members of `SpaceState` minus `entries`; an optional member that is absent is omitted, never `null`. Every claim in it carries `settled` (D14): `true` once its outcome or an `effect.resolve` settled it (P-06: the reservation, if any, was released or consumed; a claim without a reservation is settled by the same events), `false` before. A claim whose outcome was the executor's own `unknown` stays `settled: false` (its reservation stays charged) unless the effect is repeatable.

**P-01 Relevant actors.** `relevantActors(state, entry, ctx)` is the whole pin rule: the actors whose profiles the receipt pins, each once, at its first position, in this order: the author; the target of a `member.set`; the targets of an instruction (the actor of the session a `session.input` steers, each assignee of a `task`, the executor of an `effect.propose`); the agent of an `agent.unguard`; for an `effect.approve`, `effect.cancel` or `effect.claim` of a proposal in the state, its proposer, its executor and the agents in its `guardedFor`; for an approve while `forceGuarded` is on, then the authors of its unconsumed approvals (the cap counts none that is forced, P-08); for a claim then its guards, the operators in the profiles in `ctx.profiles` of the agents whose guard must approve it (P-15), and the approval *candidates* (E3, unexpired at `ctx.time`) in candidate order up to and including the first that is *usable* by the profiles in `ctx.profiles`: a valid key, and an author who isn't guarded (P-15; every candidate before it, unknown, revoked or guarded, included, since its profile decides). A guard is decided from the profile pinned for **this** entry, so there is no state snapshot of anyone's operator. The sequencer holds none of this: it calls the function with the profiles it has fetched for the entry so far (none at first), fetches every actor named that it has not tried, and calls again until no new actor is named. That is at most three calls: the first names the actors the entry names and every candidate; the second adds the guards from those actors' profiles and ends the candidates at the first usable one; the third names nothing new. Every call has `ctx.time` = the append's one `now`, which is also Policy's `ctx.time` (§4.1 step 5) and the receipt time. It pins exactly the last list, never an actor it only tried on an earlier call, and answers `unavailable` if an actor on the last list could not be fetched. So the keys of `receipt.profiles` are exactly `relevantActors(state, entry, {time: receipt.time, profiles: the pinned views})`, which a verifier checks (R-14; wire vector `probes-r27.json::N3a`). So an unreachable approver matters only if every earlier approver's key was revoked (wire scenario "approvers are pinned up to the first valid key"). `decide` needs the actors the entry names in `ctx.profiles`, and a candidate or guard only where E3 reaches it (P-16). At most 36 approvals are unconsumed per proposal and an effect's `guardedFor` holds at most 19 agents (P-08, P-15), which bounds `receipt.profiles` (a `task` adds at most its 64 assignees, an approve at most those 36 authors).


**P-16 `invalid` and `denied`, and the order of checks.** `decide` answers `allow`, `denied` or `invalid` (han.ts `Decision`). The codes are defined by the **class of the check that failed**, not by whether a later entry could change the answer. Neither can be retried (§3); only `unavailable` means "same request later".
- **`invalid`**: the entry is malformed or inconsistent with what it names. That is: its shape is wrong (R-09); it names something that isn't there, or is there as another kind (a `task` that is a message counts as missing); it contradicts what it names (a different `planDigest`, a `(result, started)` pair outside the table, a cost the claim's reservation can't cover); its place is impossible (`seen` or `checkpoint.through` beyond the head, a `space.create` on an existing space, a first entry that isn't one, a reused keyId, a `key.revoke` of a key that isn't valid); or the caller left a required profile out of `ctx` (R-25). `seen` > head is `invalid` although the head grows: a sequencer orders an entry only against its head at append, so an entry claiming to have seen records that don't exist yet is impossible at that moment, and the sender signs a new one once they do.
- **`denied`**: an authority, role, phase, time, budget or policy check failed: the key, role, kind, server or accountability of the author; the policy; the phase or flags of the task or effect; time (expiry, the abandon wait); budgets; counts and caps. This includes permanent failures, such as a revoked key, a closed task, a final phase or an expired proposal. A sequencer answers an abandon that is merely early `unavailable` (R-16), but Policy's own verdict on it is `denied`.
- **Order.** The verdict is that of the first failing check. (Policy is step 5 of the Log's one order, §4.1: the signature is judged before it, and rate limits and the moment-dependent `unavailable` after it.) All kinds share the common steps below, then follow the kind's own list. The kind lists are in the order `ref/policy.py` runs them, and where they interleave `invalid` and `denied` the order is part of the rule (vectors `decision-order` and `kind-check-order`).

Common steps:
1. Shape of the header fields Policy reads and of the body (R-25, R-09): `invalid`.
2. Pins: every actor the entry names (the author, a `member.set` target, the targets of an instruction, an unguard's agent, the proposer, executor and `guardedFor` of the proposal an approve, cancel or claim names, and for an approve while `forceGuarded` is on the authors of its unconsumed approvals) has a view in `ctx.profiles`; a claim's candidates and guards are checked when E3 reaches them: else `invalid`.
3. The author's key is `valid` in `profiles[author]` (R-12): else `denied`.
4. The space has no state yet: only `space.create` is possible (else `invalid`). In a profile space, the author must be `@:<server of the actor>` (`denied`), the body needs `profile` (`invalid`), an operator if the profile isn't human (`invalid`), and an operator other than the actor (`invalid`). In a plain space the body must not carry `profile` (`invalid`).
5. The space exists: a `space.create` is `invalid`; `seen` > head is `invalid`; a `refs` entry not in the space is `invalid` (R-05).
6. The author's server is in `policy.servers.deny` and the kind isn't `effect.outcome` or `effect.resolve`: `denied`.
7. In a profile space: `key.add`, `key.revoke`, `member.set` follow the table, every other kind is `denied`.
8. The author is guarded (P-15) and the entry is an `effect.approve`, a `member.set` other than leaving (`{actor: author, role: "none"}`), or a `session.input` into a session whose actor is another: `denied`.
9. An extension kind: `denied` unless the author's role is at least `extensionsMinRole`. Nothing else is checked.
10. The kind's own list.

In the kind lists, **I** is `invalid`, **D** is `denied`, and a check that allows ends the list. "No role" means the author doesn't hold owner, member or guest. "Accountable" is P-03. A role named here is the author's *current* role.

### 6.2 Roles, joining, reading

Roles: `owner`, `member`, `guest`, `banned`, or none (absent). **Owners are human.** Only actors of kind `human` may hold `owner`; agents and services are members at most.

**P-02 Read.** `canRead(state, reader, seq)` is true if:
- `policy.visibility = "public"`; or
- `reader` is owner/member/guest now; or
- `reader` held such a role at `seq`, meaning former members keep what they saw: `roleHistory` has an interval `{from, to?}` with `from ≤ seq < to` (no `to` while the role lasts). `from` is the seq of the `member.set` that gave a readable role to an actor with none (or banned), the creator's is 1, and `to` is the seq of the `member.set` that ended it (to `banned` or `none`). So a removed member reads what came before the removal, not the removal entry itself. Moving between readable roles opens no interval. A role held **now** reads every seq, the ones before it was granted included. **`roleHistory` keeps at most the 64 most recent intervals of each actor**: when an actor is given a readable role for the 65th time, the fold drops its oldest interval first. "Held such a role at `seq`" is therefore *some kept interval contains `seq`*. Trimming only ever closes: it changes the answer for a former member only at a `seq` before the `from` of its oldest kept interval, where the answer becomes *not readable*; for every `seq` at or after that `from`, and for every actor that holds a role now, the answer is the same as without the cap. (This keeps the state bounded however often members come and go; a member who left and came back more than 64 times loses the oldest stretches it once read); or
- `reader` is a server actor `@:S`, and either `S` is the space's current home, or `S` is not in `policy.servers.deny` and some actor on `S` holds a readable role now or at `seq` (as above).

Profile spaces are public. The call `state` needs read rights at the head, or at `at` when `at` is given (§3). **Read rights are always evaluated on the current state** (`canRead(state, reader, seq)` with the state at the head): a role held now reads every seq, so a member who joined at seq 3 can read `state {at: 2}` and the records before their join, and a reader with no role now and none at `seq` reads nothing (wire scenario "a role held now reads the state at an earlier seq"). The state returned for `at` is the historical fold; the rights are not. On a transport, chat visibility is the transport's business, and HAN's read rights govern only the HAN log.

**P-03 Membership** (`member.set {actor, role}` by `author`, with target's current role `cur`):
- An **owner** may set any role (their own included), except that a set leaving zero owners is denied (even when an owner demotes or bans themself), and a non-human can't be made owner.
- **Self-leave:** an owner, member or guest may set themselves to none (the last owner can't, above). A banned actor may not.
- **Open join:** if `join = "open"`, an actor with `cur` = none may set themselves to `member` (not `guest`). Banned actors may not.
- **Invite:** if `membersMayInvite`, a member (not a guest) may set an actor with `cur` = none to `guest` or `member`.
- Anything else is denied. `member.set` is never `invalid` once its shape is right.
- **Agents:** when a readable role is *granted* to a target whose profile `actorKind ≠ "human"` and who holds none (no role, or banned), `allowAgents = "none"` denies, and `"with-operator"` requires `operatorConfirmed`. Moving between readable roles, banning and removing are never re-checked. The same check, called *accountability*, is re-applied to a non-human author at every `effect.propose`, `effect.claim` and budgeted `task`, and to the *proposer* at claim time (E2). So withdrawing an operator's confirmation, or switching `allowAgents` to `"none"`, stops agents already in the space.
- **Servers:** *granting* a readable role to an actor on a denied server is denied. Banning or removing such an actor is always allowed.
- **Profile spaces:** the actor and `@:server` are the owners, fixed at creation, and `roles` never changes afterwards. The only `member.set`s allowed are (a) the **confirmation**: author = the profile's `operator`, body `{actor: operator, role: "owner"}`, and the operator is `human` in `ctx.profiles`; and (b) the **withdrawal**: author = the operator or `@:server`, body `{actor: operator, role: "none"}`. Each sets `operatorConfirmed` to `true` or `false`. Everything else is denied, including the actor's own and the server's confirmation, and any `member.set` in a profile with no operator. The `operator` itself is fixed at creation: there is no operator change, only confirmation and withdrawal. §4.3.
- **Creation.** `space.create` makes its author the owner (roleHistory `[{from: 1}]`) and `budgetLeft` the whole budget. Owners are human, so a creator that isn't human is `denied`; a policy whose `servers.deny` names the space's own home is `invalid`. Whether to host a space at all is still the sequencer's decision (§4.1 step 2). A fork's `forkOf` is checked for its shape only.

**P-04 Who may append what** (beyond the kind-specific rules below). "Holding a role" means owner, member or guest *now*. Approvers must also hold one. Authors on a denied server may append nothing, except fact reports (`effect.outcome`, `effect.resolve`).

| `task` | owner, member; or an assignee of `parent` who still holds a role |
| `task.update` | an assignee of `task` who still holds a role; or an owner, with a final state only (force-close) |

| `key.add`, `key.revoke` | profile spaces only (§4.3) |
| `space.create` | never in an existing space (`invalid`, P-16) |

### 6.3 Tasks and the budget

A space has at most **one budget**, `policy.budget = {unit, amount}`, or `null`. Amounts are integers in minor units. `budgetLeft` is the budget minus everything *committed* (reserved or spent). Amounts only move between the space, task budgets and claim reservations, or are spent at settlement. The fuzzer checks that task remainders never go negative, that no remainder exceeds its budget, and that the space remainder goes negative only through a cut.

**P-05 Tasks.**
- `to` is non-empty (an empty list is `invalid`) and every assignee currently holds a role (`denied` if not).
- A `parent` must be an open task in this space (an id that isn't a task is `invalid`), and the author must be one of its assignees: a guest assignee may delegate, an owner who isn't an assignee may not. Top-level tasks have depth 0; a child's depth is its parent's + 1, which must be ≤ `maxDelegationDepth` (so 0 forbids delegation).
- **Task budgets** exist only in budgeted spaces and use the space's unit. A task's budget is reserved from its parent if the parent has a budget, otherwise from the space, and must fit what is left there. A child of a budgeted parent MUST carry a budget. An agent or service may create a budgeted task only as a child of an open budgeted task assigned to it.
- A final `task.update` (`done`, `failed`, `declined`, `cancelled`) closes the task and refunds its unspent remainder to the nearest *open* budgeted ancestor, starting at its parent, or to the space if there is none. The closed task's own remainder becomes 0. An assignee can't close a task while an effect naming it is `claimed` or `unknown`. An **owner who is not an assignee** may close any open task with a final update (force-close), for example when its assignees are gone, even over a claimed effect; an owner who is an assignee closes under the assignee rule.
- On a closed task, these are refused: `task.update`, `task.cancel`, a child `task`, and an `effect.propose` that names it.
- `task.cancel` sets `cancelRequested`, and may be repeated. The task stays open until an assignee, or an owner, posts a final update.

**P-06 Costs.**
- **Budgeted spaces are closed.** If `policy.budget` is set, every `effect.propose` MUST carry a `cost` in its unit, and a claim of an effect without one is denied (E6). An outcome or resolve may report a cost only within its claim's reservation (the same unit, an amount no larger). With no reservation, it may report a cost only in an unbudgeted space, where costs are informational. Any other cost is `invalid`, not `denied` (P-16), because the reservation is fixed by the claim.
- **Only humans draw on the space budget.** In a budgeted space, an agent or service MUST name, in every proposal, an open budgeted task assigned to it, and its costs are charged there. E6 checks it again at claim, so a budget added after an agent's proposal still can't be spent by it. Agents can't be owners (§6.2), so they can't change the budget either.
- **Naming a task:** an `effect.propose` that names a task is `denied` unless its author is an assignee of that task or an owner, whether or not the task has a budget.
- **Charging:** an effect's cost goes to its named task if that task has a budget when the claim is ordered, otherwise to the space. It is reserved at claim and settled at outcome or resolve. Settling with a cost refunds the difference, and a settlement with no cost charges the whole reservation. A refund goes to the claim's source task if that task is still open and budgeted, otherwise to the nearest open budgeted ancestor of it, otherwise to the space (as in P-05).
- **Changing the budget** (`policy.set`): with the same unit, `budgetLeft` = new amount − committed, which can go negative after a cut. Nothing new can be reserved until refunds bring it back up. Changing the unit, or adding or removing the budget, is allowed only while nothing is reserved, meaning no open task holds a budget and no claim holds an unsettled reservation (money already spent isn't reserved); the remainder then starts fresh.

### 6.4 The effect state machine

Phases: `open`, `claimed`, `unknown`, and the final phases `succeeded`, `failed`, `cancelled`. `cancelRequested` is a flag. A final phase never changes.

**Transition table.** Any (phase × kind) combination not listed is `denied`.

| Phase | `effect.approve` | `effect.cancel` | `effect.claim` | `effect.outcome` | `effect.resolve` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| open | stays open (adds an approval) | → cancelled (sets `cancelRequested`) | → claimed (P-07) | — | — |
| claimed | stays claimed | sets `cancelRequested` (denied if already set) | — | per P-09 | — |
| unknown | — | sets `cancelRequested` (denied if already set) | — | — | per P-10 |
| final | — | — | — | — | — |

`effect.propose` → `open`. **No readiness is stored.** Whether a claim may proceed (approvals in hand, the guards' included, P-15) is decided by E3 when the claim is made; the executor claims and lets Policy judge, at most once per head per effect, since each refused claim costs a journal write, a signature and a pass of the pipeline (X-09). After an attempt ends without success and the effect may run again, its phase is `cancelled` if `cancelRequested`, else `open` again.

**P-07 Claim** (`effect.claim {proposal, attempt, instance, window}`). Allowed only when all of these hold:
- **E1** The author is the proposal's `executor`, still holds owner or member, is accountable (P-03), and `instance` is the valid keyId that signed this claim.
- **E2** The proposer still holds owner/member, is still accountable (P-03), and the key that signed the proposal is still valid in the proposer's pinned profile.
- **E3** If approval is needed, walk the **candidates**: unconsumed approvals in order whose `expiresAt` (if any) is after `ctx.time`, whose author satisfies the approvers rule *now*, and (with `separateApprover`) who aren't the proposer. The first candidate that is **usable** is consumed: its signing key is valid and its author isn't guarded at the claim (P-15; whether it is forced, the profile pinned for the claim decides). Candidates with revoked keys or guarded authors are skipped, so an approval made before its author was guarded counts for nothing once it is. If none is usable, deny. Then each guard P-15 requires (of the agents in `guardedFor`, and of the proposer and the executor if guarded at the claim) needs an unconsumed, unexpired approval by itself, signed with a valid key, while it holds a role; a single approval by a guard who is also a rule approver satisfies both (the claim records it as `approval` and again in `guardApprovals`; it is consumed once); an agent with no confirmed operator can't be claimed for, and a guard whose profile can't be fetched makes the claim `unavailable` (P-01).
- **E4** `cancelRequested` is false. (Redundant with the table, kept so one rule states it.)
- **E5** The phase is `open`; `attempt` = 1 + the number of earlier claims; `window` = the current `policy.launchWindow`; and the proposal's `expiresAt`, if any, is after `ctx.time`; and the task it names, if any, is still open; and fewer than `MAX_ATTEMPTS` (64, han.ts `PolicyConstants`) claims precede it, so the 65th is `denied` ("attempt limit: propose again") and the executor proposes anew.
- **E6** In a budgeted space, the effect has a cost in the space's unit that fits what is left where P-06 charges it. It is then reserved.

The claim records its receipt time and pins its `window`.

**P-08 Approvals.** A client MUST display the plan whose `planDigest` it is about to sign, and MUST NOT offer an approval for a plan it hasn't fetched and hash-checked. `policy.approvals` is a list of `{action, approvers}`. The first entry whose `action` equals the proposal's action, or is `"*"`, applies, and `approvers` is a non-empty list of roles (`owner`, `member`, `guest`) or actor IDs. A role entry matches an actor whose current role is exactly that role (an owner doesn't satisfy `["member"]`; list both to allow both), an ID entry matches that actor, and either way the approver must hold a role now. If none applies, no approval is needed. With `separateApprover: true`, the proposer can't approve their own proposal. An approval for a proposal not in this space, or with a wrong `planDigest`, is `invalid`. An approval is accepted while the effect is `open` or `claimed`, and `denied` in any other phase. An approval whose `expiresAt` has already passed (at or before `ctx.time`) is denied. A new approval **supersedes** its author's open ones on the same proposal (they are dropped; a consumed one stays consumed), so approvals can be refreshed, and each author has at most one open. An approval is **open** while it is unconsumed and unexpired. Whenever any entry touches an effect, the fold drops its unconsumed approvals whose `expiresAt` is at or before `ctx.time`, so the state holds no lapsed approval and its author loses the right to cancel with it. **The cap counts only approvals that can still count.** A new approval by one of the effect's guards (P-15: the guard of an agent in its `guardedFor`, or of its proposer or executor if guarded; judged with the profiles pinned for the new approval) is never refused for the cap. Any other is `denied` when 16 *countable* open approvals (unconsumed and unexpired) by other authors exist (`MAX_OPEN_APPROVALS`): an approval counts while its author holds a role, isn't guarded (by choice, or forced by the profile pinned for the new approval, which P-01 pins) and isn't one of the effect's guards. Expired ones don't count (vector `approval-cap-counts-open-approvals`). So guards are never crowded out (vectors `guards-are-never-crowded-out`, `a-full-guard-chain-is-claimable`), nor is anyone by approvals that can never be consumed (`cap-counts-only-countable-approvals`). **Dropping.** The fold drops an author's unconsumed approvals on every effect when it loses its role (`member.set` to `none` or `banned`) or chooses guarding, and drops one a not-started outcome returns (P-09) if its author has since done either; an approval's fold also drops the open approvals of authors guarded by the profiles pinned for it (forcing is judged per entry: switching it on drops nothing, and until the next approval, switching it off makes them count again). A dropped approval never comes back, and its cancel right goes with it (vector `lost-roles-drop-approvals`). Key revocation is not visible in the fold: an approval signed with a since-revoked key still counts toward the cap until it expires or is superseded, and its author supersedes it by approving again with a valid key (vector `revoked-keys-still-count-until-superseded`). **Bounds** (derived, han.ts `PolicyConstants`; the fuzzer asserts each): an author holds at most one unconsumed and one consumed approval on an effect. Authors that can never be its guards hold at most 17 (the cap, plus one that a not-started outcome returns: every approval's fold leaves only countable ones besides the guards'); its possible guards are the operators of the agents in `guardedFor` and of the proposer and executor, at most 19, since an operator never changes (§4.3). So an effect holds at most 36 unconsumed approvals (`MAX_UNCONSUMED_APPROVALS`) and 55 in all (`MAX_APPROVALS`).

**P-09 Outcome** (`effect.outcome {claim, result, started, cost?}` for the latest claim, at most once per claim: it needs phase `claimed`, so an older claim, or one that already has an outcome, is `denied`; an id that is no claim is `invalid`):
- **Author:** normally the claim's instance, i.e. the same actor with `keyId = claim.instance`.
- **Abandon:** an owner may append it with `result: "unknown"` and `started: "unknown"` only if `ctx.time ≥ claim.time + A`, where `A = ⌈claim.window · 101 / 99⌉ + 2` computed in integers, and the sequencer's steady clock agrees (R-16). For example, 30 s gives 33 s and 3600 s gives 3675 s. An abandon by anyone but an owner, or before `A`, is `denied` by Policy (a sequencer answers an owner's early abandon `unavailable` with `retryAfterMs` only when Policy would allow it at claim time + `A`, R-16). An abandon always leaves the effect `unknown`, even if it is repeatable, because it might still be running. An outcome counts as the executor's own report only when signed with the claim's `instance` key; any other key, even the executor's, is an abandon and leaves `unknown`.
- **`started: "no"`** (result `failed` or `cancelled`): the consumed approvals and the cost reservation are returned. A returned approval is **dropped** if its author has approved again since (a newer open approval on the same proposal supersedes it, P-08), or has since lost its role or chosen guarding (P-08). The phase is then `cancelled` if `cancelRequested`, else `open` again.
- **`started: "yes"`:** cost settles at `cost`, which must be ≤ the reservation (default: the reservation), and the rest is refunded. `succeeded` → phase `succeeded`. `failed`, or `cancelled` (stopped partway) → phase `failed`; but if the effect is `repeatable` and no cancel is requested, → `open` again instead: the consumed approvals stay consumed, so a claim needs a fresh one.
- **The executor's own `unknown`:** the reservation stays charged and the phase becomes `unknown`. If `repeatable` and no cancel is requested, the reservation is instead settled as spent in full and the phase is `open` again: the proposer declared that running it again is harmless.

Valid `(result, started)` pairs:

| result \ started | `yes` | `no` | `unknown` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `succeeded` | ✓ | — | — |
| `failed` | ✓ (ran and failed) | ✓ (never launched) | — |
| `cancelled` | ✓ (stopped partway) | ✓ (never launched) | — |
| `unknown` | ✓ (launched, result lost) | — | ✓ |

All other pairs are `invalid`. `cost` is allowed only with `started: "yes"` (else `invalid`), and must lie within the reservation (P-06).

**P-10 Resolve** (`effect.resolve {proposal, result, cost?}` with evidence, in phase `unknown`):
- **Author:** the proposal's executor (any instance), or an owner.
- `succeeded` → phase `succeeded`, with cost settled as in P-09. `failed` → the reservation is refunded, every unconsumed approval is dropped (none is carried), and the phase becomes `cancelled` if `cancelRequested`, otherwise `open` again. A `cost` with `failed` is `invalid`.
- Resolving is a statement of fact, and the *only* way a non-repeatable effect with an uncertain past can run again. `unknown` is reached only by the executor's report, or by an abandon after the claim's window, so the claim can no longer launch. A resolve that is true when made therefore never leads to a second run (model property UnknownNeverSilentlyRetried, which counts only resolves already false when made).

### 6.5 Redaction, checkpoints, forks

**P-11 Kind-specific checks.**
- `checkpoint.through` ≤ the current head, else `invalid`; the author is an owner or a member.
- `space.move` is denied while any effect is `claimed`, so one sequencer orders a claim and its abandon (R-16). A move to the current home is allowed.
- `policy.set` replaces the whole policy, applying the P-06 budget rules. It can't change the `window` pinned in existing claims. It can't put the current home's server in `servers.deny`, and can't leave every current owner on a denied server (denying some owners' servers is allowed). Roles are untouched, because roles live only in `member.set`.
- `space.create` is only valid as the first entry: in a space that has any entry it is `invalid`, and the first entry of a space must be one (P-16).

**P-12 Redact** (`redact {target}`): the target's content is deleted wherever honoured, its `redacted` flag set and its blob references released. The target must be in the space (`invalid`). A `redact` can't target another `redact`. The plan of an unfinished effect can't be redacted (cancel it first). The author must be the target's author, whatever role they hold now, or an owner. Both refusals are `denied`.

**P-13 Forks carry no authority.** A space with `forkOf {space, through}` starts from its own `space.create` alone: creator as owner, the new policy, and nothing carried over (no members, tasks, approvals, budget commitments or effects). The parent's records are history to read, never authority. Policy checks `forkOf` for its shape only.

### 6.6 Live sessions and steering

The point of a space is to see and steer work outside your own silo while it happens, not after a PR. A **session** is one actor's live working context (a Claude Code run, an editor, a human at a terminal). Its stream travels on the transport (T-07); the log holds the authority over it.

**P-15 Session rights and steering.**
- **Sessions.** `session.open {steerMode, task?}` opens a session whose actor is the author: any role holder, accountable (P-03), at most 64 open per actor. `steerMode` (`auto` or `guarded`) must be in `policy.sessions.modes`, which limits nothing else. A `task` must be open and assigned to the author (or the author is an owner). An agent may open a `guarded` session only if its guard holds a role here. `session.close` (its actor or an owner) removes the session from the state. A session is **live** while it is open and its actor holds a role.
- **Rights.** `policy.sessions = {watch, read, steer, modes, forceGuarded?}`; the first three are each a minimum role (`guest`, `member`, `owner`, `nobody`), `read` and `steer` no wider than `watch`. `sessionAccess(state, actor, session)` returns three booleans: on a live session its actor has all three; anyone else has a right if their role is at least its level and the level isn't `nobody`; a dead session grants nothing. **Watch** = the output and status stream; **read** = its full context; **steer** = append `session.input`.
- **Guarded.** A non-human X is guarded if it chose to be (it opened a `guarded` session and joined the set `guarded`) or is **forced** (`forceGuarded` is on). Its **guard** is the confirmed `operator` in the profile pinned for the entry. `guardStatus(state, ctx, X)` returns `{guard?, guarded, forced}`. Only X's guard, holding a role, ends X's choice, with `agent.unguard {agent: X}`.
- **Recorded on the work.** Every task and effect carries `guardedFor`, fixed at creation: a task's is its parent's plus its author if guarded; an effect's is its task's, then that of every other open task listing its proposer as an assignee (named or not), plus its proposer and executor if guarded (a forced service executor too: its operator must approve). Over 19 agents is `denied` ("too many guarded tasks; close some"). Nothing frees it: not an unguard, nor switching forcing off. So a guarded agent may delegate: whatever its delegates, agent or human, propose waits for its guard until the task closes; that is the cost (a parentless subtask: §7.5).
- **Claims.** E3 (P-07) also requires, for each agent in `guardedFor` and the proposer and executor if guarded at the claim, an unconsumed, unexpired approval by that agent's guard, signed with a valid key, while the guard holds a role; an agent with no confirmed operator can't be claimed for. Guards may approve (exempt from `separateApprover` and the cap, P-08) and cancel; nobody stands in for a guard, owners included. An approval whose author is guarded counts for no rule, nor toward the cap, and grants no cancel right (P-08, P-04).
- **Limits.** A guarded author may not approve, govern or steer others: its `effect.approve`, `member.set` (except leaving: its own role to `none`) and `session.input` into another's session are `denied`; everything else is allowed. Instructions to a guarded agent are allowed and inert: nothing they cause runs without its guard, and hosts run them in the stricter mode (R-24).

Other channels, other spaces and actions inside the session's sandbox are outside what Policy gates (§7.5).

## 7. Verification and trust

### 7.1 Who is trusted for what

Anyone may run a sequencer or a verifier. A space's policy decides which actors may act. Its first sequencer is named in its space id, and later ones by signed moves. The transport is trusted only to deliver bytes; every HAN fact is signed, chained and replayable.

### 7.2 Consistency

- **Within a space:** one total order (seq), verifiable by anyone who holds the records (R-14).
- **Across spaces:** no order. Links are content only (R-05).
- **Availability (a stated trade-off):** while a space's sequencer is unreachable, no HAN entry can be appended. Chat on the transport continues. This is the price of one answer to "was this authorized?".

### 7.3 Moves

The handshake can't use profile-space signals (G-04 lets those carry only notices), and the target B has no read rights in the space yet. It uses two things: signals in the space being moved, between its owners and its current home A, and a server-to-server call `host` from A to B (§8.3, han.ts `BindingCalls`).

1. **Request.** An owner signals in the space, `{type: "host-request", to: B}` (T-05). Only readers can signal (G-04), and A, as home, can read the space as `@:A`. A dials B over the generic binding, authenticated as `@:A` (`hello`), and calls `host {space, records, head}`, pushing the chain in batches. B verifies every record (R-14), fetching the profile spaces it needs, which are public. B answers `{have}` (the highest contiguous seq it holds) until it holds the chain through `head`, A's current head.
2. **Accept.** If B is willing, its answer to the call that completes the chain through `head` also carries `accept = {keyId, sig}`, with `sig = Ed25519(B's key, "han-accept-move-v1\n" ‖ space ‖ "\n" ‖ receiptHash(head))`; `keyId` names a key in B's `/.well-known/han`. If B is unwilling the call fails `denied`, and if the chain fails R-14 it fails `invalid`. A signals the owners in the space, `{type: "host-accept", to: B, head, keyId, sig}`; they read it as ordinary readers.
3. **Move.** The owner appends `space.move {to: B, accept}`. A verifies `accept` against its *current* head. If the head moved since B signed, the move is `invalid`, and the owner repeats from step 1. The move is denied while an effect is `claimed` (P-11). A orders it at seq `n`. That is A's last record until a later move names A again; from then on A answers `moved`.
4. **Continue.** A pushes the records after `head` through `n` to B with `host` (without `head`). B, holding the chain through `n` whose last record moves the space to B, orders the next entry at `n+1`, with `prev = receiptHash(n)`, signed by B, and with a time ≥ A's last (it waits if its clock is behind, R-03a). Until then B is not a home: it answers every call that names the space `moved`, with `movedTo` = A (§4.1 step 2). B reaches the chain only through A's `host` calls (G-06); it needs no read of its own, and it publishes the key behind `accept.keyId` in its `/.well-known/han`.

Verifiers accept records above `n` only when they are signed by B (R-02) and the move carries a valid `accept` (R-14).

### 7.4 When a sequencer dies

HAN v1 has **no forced takeover**. Any rule letting someone other than the home keep writing a space reopens double-execution and rollback attacks; three review rounds showed this. Instead:
- The log stays **verifiable** at every verifier, with its profiles and server keys (§4.3).
- Owners continue in a **fork** (`space.create {forkOf}`) on any willing sequencer. A fork carries **no authority** (P-13). Effects that were open in the dead space are, honestly, of unknown fate. Re-doing one is a new proposal that a human decides to make.
- Forced takeover is left to an extension, which must show that the model properties still hold.

### 7.5 Trust assumptions, and what each misbehaviour is caught by

| Misbehaviour | Caught by |
|---|---|
| Sequencer accepts a forbidden entry | verifier replay (R-14) |
| Sequencer shows different histories to different parties | head gossip (R-15) → equivocation proof (R-02) |
| A server signs a seq when it isn't the home there | R-14 → wrong-home proof (R-02) |
| Sequencer uses stale profiles (ignores a recent key revocation) | partly: monotonicity is checked (R-10); 300 s freshness is trusted |
| Sequencer clock jumps forward | R-03a steady clock + R-16 elapsed-time abandon; verifiers flag receipts dated in the future. A sequencer that *lies* about time is trusted for clock rate within 1%. |
| Sequencer serves a record as redacted without a `redact` entry | R-14: a redaction must carry its signed proof |
| Owner moves a space to a sequencer that never agreed | §7.3: the move needs the target's signed acceptance of the current head |
| A server adds a key to one of its users' profiles (impersonation) | not prevented: users trust their own server for account recovery. The added key is public in the profile space, so the user can see it. |
| A server's retired key is stolen and used to back-date receipts | partly: verifiers keep the keys and receipts they have seen, and a `compromised` mark stops new trust; receipts recorded before the mark stay trusted |
| Sequencer refuses or delays appends, or dies | not prevented; remedy is a move, or a fork (§7.4) |
| Transport or sequencer reads plaintext | not prevented by HAN; use the transport's end-to-end encryption for content |
| An agent host acts on text it read (another session's stream or context, a message) as instruction: prompt injection | not prevented by Policy, which can't see what a model read: R-24 obliges hosts to treat it as data and SHOULD run such turns guarded. Effects it then proposes are attributable to the agent and its operator. |
| A guarded agent acts in another space, or persuades another agent through a `message` | not prevented: guarding is per space and messages are data. R-24 keeps the taint per agent (an outside instruction in any session, in any space, makes the agent's later turns strict once it is guarded) and obliges hosts to treat messages as data; the operator answers for it. |
| Work created before an agent became guarded | recorded as it was: `guardedFor` is fixed at creation (P-15). Its claim still needs the guard of its proposer and executor if they are guarded at the claim; a finished effect is history. |
| An unguard or switching `forceGuarded` off frees work a guarded agent proposed or delegated (round 26's N1) | prevented (P-15): `guardedFor` never shrinks. An operator never changes (§4.3); while it is withdrawn, nothing recorded for its agent can be claimed. |
| A guarded agent's delegate does delegated work Policy can't tie to the task: a subtask it creates without a `parent`, and the work of whoever that subtask is assigned to; or the delegate closes the task (a signed `task.update`) and works on | not prevented by Policy, which can't see what drove an entry. Everything else a delegate proposes while it is assigned the task, agent or human, task named or not, carries the delegator (P-15; round 28's S1). R-24 obliges every agent's host to name the driving task as the `parent` of its subtasks and to run strict every turn a guarded author or a task with a `guardedFor` drives; nothing covers a human delegate's parentless subtask beyond its own judgement; the approval rules still apply to all. |
| Approvals that can never be consumed fill the cap, so a proposal can't be approved (round 28's S2) | prevented (P-08): only countable approvals count, and the fold drops those of authors that lose their role or choose guarding. Accepted: an approval signed with a since-revoked key counts until it expires or its author supersedes it |
| A guarded agent cancels another's effect with an approval it made before it was guarded (S7) | prevented (P-04): a guarded author's approval grants no cancel right |
| An agent approves while unguarded, is then guarded (by its choice or by forcing), and its old approval is used | prevented (P-07 E3): an approval whose author is guarded at the claim is never consumed |
| An agent takes an outside instruction before it is guarded, and acts on it after | not prevented by Policy: R-24 obliges hosts to mark every session that took an outside instruction, guarded or not, and to run its turns strict once the agent is guarded |
| A steered (prompt-injected) agent loosens its own guarding: closes its guarded session (closing again is `invalid`), or unguards itself | prevented (P-15): closing never unguards and an agent's own unguard is denied; only its guard ends the choice, with a signed `agent.unguard`. Owners may additionally guard every non-human with `forceGuarded`, session or not, whatever it calls itself. |
| An agent whose profile server registers it as `human` escapes forcing and has no guard | accepted (trust is per space): `actorKind` is a claim made by the actor's own profile server, and Policy believes it. Owners decide whom to admit; `forceGuarded` does not protect against a profile server that lies |
| An agent its operator confirms after it joined, a role change while the operator withdrew, or a model labelled `service` escapes `forceGuarded` | prevented (P-15): forcing is decided per entry from the profile pinned for it, with no state snapshot; a forced agent with no confirmed operator may be instructed, but nothing recorded for it can be claimed |
| A harness's "stricter mode" lets a risky local action through | not prevented: only effects are gated by HAN. Local actions within the session's sandbox are the harness's (§8.4). |
| The transport delivers a stream to someone without the right | Matrix binding: per-session, per-right rooms whose membership the sequencer keeps equal to `sessionAccess` (§8.2). With E2EE (RECOMMENDED) the sequencer's room user has no device and can't decrypt; without it the publisher waits until membership matches and the homeserver can read the items. A homeserver admin who adds devices or users is out of scope, as for chat. |

HAN v1 runs no consensus.

## 8. Transport bindings

### 8.1 What HAN requires of a transport

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **T-01 Carry the log** | Deliver every appended `LogRecord` to the space's members who may read it (P-02), in any order. A binding that enforces P-02 by pull (Matrix, §8.2) MAY deliver only a notice of the record and let the reader fetch it with `read`. Verifiers restore order from the chain, so the transport needs no ordering or integrity of its own. |
| **T-02 Map identities** | A HAN `ActorId` is the transport user with the same ID. v1 supports transport IDs that fit the ActorId grammar (§2; e.g. Matrix IDs without ports or uppercase); others need an alias profile in a later version. HAN authorship comes only from HAN signatures (R-12), never from the transport's sender field. |
| **T-03 Carry files** | Store and fetch blob bytes named by `BlobRef` (with its `uri`). Every reader MUST verify `sha256` before use. Access control to the bytes is the transport's, and a HAN reference grants no access by itself. |
| **T-04 Invite** | Let owners bring members into the room; first contact is the transport's (e.g. Matrix invites). |
| **T-05 Signal** | Carry ephemeral messages: presence, typing, streamed agent replies, verifier gossip (heads and faults, R-15), and an owner's request for a move and the answer (§7.3). Signals never carry authority: anything that matters is an entry. On the generic binding they ride `subscribe` and never `read` (G-09). |
| **T-06 Room ↔ space** | Bind each room to at most one HAN space, so members can find its sequencer. |
| **T-07 Session streams** | Carry each live session's `StreamItem`s (han.ts: output, status, context snapshots as blobs) to the actors whose `sessionAccess` grants watch, and context items only to those granted read. After a receipt removes an actor's right (a lost role, a closed session, a policy change), items the publisher sends once it has seen that receipt MUST NOT reach that actor. Publishers check `sessionAccess` at their latest receipt before each item. Streams carry no authority: a steer is a `session.input` (P-15), never a stream message. Status MAY be taken from the harness's terminal program-status reports (OSC 7501: idle, working, blocked, done, error) and is presence, never fact. |

### 8.2 Matrix binding

The room is a **notification and chat channel**; the log is served by the sequencer. This is how the binding keeps P-02: a Matrix room is visible to all its members and its history rules can't express "read now, or read what you saw while you held a role". So the room carries only what every member may see, and the content goes out through `GET /_han/v1/read`, which enforces P-02. The alternative, making room membership equal `canRead` at the head with `history_visibility: joined`, breaks on a late joiner (P-02 says a role held now reads every seq, `joined` hides what came before) and on a member removed from HAN, who keeps the whole timeline instead of the seqs they held a role for; it also forbids rooms wider than the log, such as a chat that includes guests who may not read it. A room MAY therefore hold people who can't read the log.

| HAN | Matrix |
|---|---|
| space | a room with state event `org.han.space` `{spaceId, sequencer}` (T-06), see "Trust anchor" |
| `append(entry, content)` | a member uploads the **whole entry** (header and content) with `POST /_han/v1/entry` (HAN-authenticated like the read, "HTTP read and entry" below; the sequencer holds it 10 minutes under its `id`), then posts only `org.han.entry` `{id}` to the room, so the room never carries a header, a body or content. The sequencer runs as an **application service** for its server. It sees the event, looks up the upload by `id`, runs §4.1, and answers with an `org.han.receipt` event `{receipt}` related to it (T-01). A resend of the same entry gets the same receipt (R-01), so Matrix's at-least-once delivery is harmless. If no upload is held for the `id`, the sequencer doesn't answer, and the client's resend (R-06) uploads it again and posts the `id` again. |
| the record (T-01) | the sequencer posts `org.han.receipt` and `org.han.entry` `{id, kind, author, seq}`; nothing more, so no body or content reaches a reader P-02 excludes. What does reach them is metadata, stated under "Room metadata" below. The full `LogRecord` (header, body, content, receipt) is served only by the HTTP read below, under P-02, and a verifier (R-14) works from that. |
| refusal | for a `denied`, `invalid`, `not_found` or `moved` entry, the sequencer sends `org.han.reject {id, code, reason}` (`moved` with `movedTo`), related to the entry's Matrix event; these are final. For `unavailable` it sends `org.han.reject {id, code: "unavailable", retryAfterMs}`, which is **not final**: the client keeps the entry pending, waits `retryAfterMs` and resends the same entry (R-06). `limit` gets no event: the entry stays pending and the client's R-06 resend retries. A client shows an entry as *pending* until it sees a receipt or a final reject. Interactive clients MAY append over the generic binding instead, which answers at once. |
| `read` | `GET /_han/v1/read` on the sequencer, see "HTTP read and entry". |
| signals (T-05) | room-wide signals (faults, executor progress, a move request or answer) are ordinary room events `org.han.signal {body}` that carry no authority. Executor progress uses `{type: "progress", claim, step, text}`, verifier gossip `{type: "heads", receipts}` and `{type: "fault", proof}` (R-15). Private ones (notices, G-03) travel server to server over the generic binding (§8.3). |
| verifier | any client or server that can read the space. Clients SHOULD show unverified or faulty HAN state as such. |
| redaction (P-12) | see "Redaction". |
| files (T-03) | Matrix authenticated media (`mxc://` URIs in `BlobRef.uri`), with HAN hash checks |
| invites, typing, presence, chat (T-04, T-05) | native Matrix |
| session streams (T-07) | **two rooms per session**, a *watch room* and a *read room*, created by the sequencer's application-service user on the `session.open` receipt and linked from the space room by state event `org.han.session` `{session, watch, read}` (state key = the session id; `watch` and `read` are room IDs). Power levels: the service user 100, everyone else 0; `invite`, `kick`, `ban`, `redact` and `state_default` 100; event `org.han.stream` 50, and the session's actor 50, so only it can post items. `history_visibility: joined`, `join_rule: invite`, `guest_access: forbidden`. After every receipt the service user invites or kicks so that each room's membership equals the actors `sessionAccess` grants that right on that session, and on `session.close` kicks everyone. Items are `org.han.stream` events whose content is a `StreamItem` (a context item adds `file`, the Matrix `EncryptedFile` info of its blob): output and status to the watch room, `context` to the read room. Context blobs are encrypted attachments (key in the event), so an `mxc://` URI alone reveals nothing. See "End-to-end encryption" for how the publisher keeps an item from a removed actor. Live audio and screen share use MatrixRTC in the watch room. |
| HAN roles vs Matrix power levels | independent. HAN authority comes only from the HAN log. HAN never reads power levels. Rooms SHOULD invite the people HAN made members, but needn't. |

**Trust anchor.** A client takes the sequencer for a room from the state event `org.han.space` only if all hold: the event was sent by the `sequencer` user it names; that user is `@han-seq:<server>`, with `<server>` the server named in the space id (the **first** home), a user in the exclusive namespace of that server's application service, so nobody else can act as it; and the room has no earlier binding (a room keeps its first binding). After a `space.move` the sequencer for the room is `@han-seq:<home>` for the home at that seq (R-02), which a client learns from the verified chain, not from the room state. A client honours receipts, rejects and `org.han.session` only from that user. For an entry sent to a room that has no binding yet, the client accepts a reject from the user expected for the entry's space. A forged binding, receipt or reject from a member is ignored.

**Room metadata (a trade-off, not a guarantee).** Every member of the room sees, for every entry, its `id`, `kind`, `author` and `seq`, and the receipt's hashes and time, **including a member P-02 excludes from reading that seq** (a guest, a former member, a person the room admits who holds no role). They learn that the author did something of that kind at that point of the log, never what. The binding accepts this: the room is a notification channel (above), the alternatives cost more than they protect, and the exposure is of the same kind as the one a Matrix room always has (who is talking, how often). Hashing the author with a per-space salt was rejected: a salt the room's members do not hold makes the field useless to the clients that show "who" and "what" in the timeline, and a salt they do hold hides nothing from them. A space that must not show this to a reader keeps that reader out of the room, or uses the generic binding, whose `subscribe` delivers a record only to readers of its seq (G-09). Content, bodies, `refs`, `seen`, `to` and budget amounts are never in the room.

**Size.** A Matrix event is limited to 64 KiB (65,536 bytes of event JSON). The only HAN events a member sends carry an `id` and the sequencer's carry an `id`, a kind, an author and a `seq` or a receipt, all far below the limit. The entry itself travels in the body of `POST /_han/v1/entry`, which is bounded by `maxFrameBytes` (the same 1.25 MiB as a generic frame) and by the header and content limits of §2; an entry too big for those is `invalid` as everywhere, with its bulk moved into a blob referenced from small content (§5).

**Redaction.** The room never carried a header, a body or content, so a `redact` has nothing in the room to erase: the events about the target hold only ids, the kind, the author, a `seq` and a receipt, which R-04 keeps anyway, and a Matrix redaction (which strips a whole event) is not used. On a `redact` receipt the sequencer deletes the content from its store, and an upload it still holds for the target; the HTTP read then serves the record as `redacted` with `redactedBy`, from which clients rebuild the proof. The room never carries the proof.

**HTTP read and entry.** The sequencer serves four endpoints at `https://<server>/_han/v1/`, `<server>` being the home's name (`dialed`):
- `GET challenge` answers a `Challenge` (han.ts): `{challenge, server, versions, time}`. A challenge is single-use and valid for 120 s (the generic binding's, G-08, is 60 s). A server MUST bound the unused challenges it holds per client address and per HAN actor, and answers a client over the bound `limit` (HTTP 429 with `Retry-After`, below) instead of issuing more.
- `GET read?space=<SpaceId>&after=<Seq>[&limit=<n>]` answers `{items: ReadItem[]}`, with the semantics of `read` (§3) without `follow` and `knownHeads`; the same R-15 head exchange is available over the generic binding. The request carries `Authorization: HAN <actor> <keyId> <proof>` and `HAN-Challenge: <challenge>`. The `HAN-Challenge` header **names the challenge** that was signed (it is mandatory), `proof = Ed25519(key, "han-hello-v1\n" ‖ challenge ‖ "\n" ‖ <server>)` exactly as in `hello`, and the key is the actor's key `keyId` in its profile (R-12). A missing, used, expired or unknown challenge, or a bad proof, is `denied`. An error is `{error: WireError}` with the HTTP status 400 `invalid`, 401 `denied`, 404 `not_found`, 421 `moved` (with `movedTo`), 429 `limit` and 503 `unavailable`. **429 and 503 carry a `Retry-After` header** (whole seconds, `retryAfterMs` rounded up, at least 1) as well as `retryAfterMs` in the body; a client that has both uses the body. A reader who may read no seq gets 404, as in §3. P-02 applies to every record.
- `GET state?space=<SpaceId>[&at=<Seq>|&cursor=<s>|&effect=<EntryId>|&agent=<ActorId>]` answers the `StateReply` `{state, next?}` of the call `state` (§3), with the same authentication, errors and selectors (the same combinations are 400 `invalid`: `at` joins `agent` only).
- `POST entry` takes `{entry, content?}` (han.ts `HttpEntryRequest`, a JSON body of at most `maxFrameBytes`), with the same `Authorization` and `HAN-Challenge` headers as the read. The authenticated actor MUST be `entry.author` (else `denied`). The server checks only that the body is an I-JSON object of exactly those members and within the size limits (`invalid`); it does not run §4.1, which happens when the room event names the `id`. It answers `200 {id}` and holds the upload for 10 minutes under its `id`; the same entry uploaded again is harmless. A server MUST bound the uploads it holds per client and per actor (count and bytes) and answers a client over the bound `limit` (429 with `Retry-After`). The upload is not a record: it is served to nobody, and it is deleted once the entry is ordered or the 10 minutes pass.

**End-to-end encryption** is RECOMMENDED for chat and for the session rooms. In session rooms the publishing host then encrypts each item only to the devices of actors `sessionAccess` grants at its latest receipt, and rotates its outbound Megolm session whenever a receipt removes any right, before sending again; the service user has no device, so it can't decrypt. Stock SDKs share room keys with every joined device, so a conforming host needs a key-sharing hook that filters recipients. **Without E2EE** the homeserver and any lagging member can read an item, so the publisher MUST NOT send an item until the room's membership matches `sessionAccess` at its latest receipt (it reads the head state over the HTTP read and waits for the kicks to land), and an unreachable service blocks publishing. Removal stops future items and keys; what someone already received stays received. The space room's `org.han.*` events can be encrypted only if the application-service user can decrypt them; otherwise the homeserver can read the ids, kinds and authors of the entries (never a body or content: the entry itself travels by HTTPS to the sequencer).

### 8.3 The generic binding

For non-Matrix use and testing, a server MAY offer the **generic binding**: one authenticated WebSocket carrying the <!--s:calls_core_w-->four<!--/s--> calls (§3) plus the binding's <!--s:binding_fns_w-->seven<!--/s--> operations (M7, han.ts `TransportBinding`) plus the server-to-server `host` (§7.3) encoded per connection: the connection authenticates `as`/`from`, `subscribe` is called `follow` in ref/wire.py, and `blobPut(bytes)` is sent as parts `blobPut(ref, offset, bytes)` of at most `maxBlobPartBytes` (G-01). The bytes on the wire are G-07 (frames) and G-08 (`hello`); G-09 says what `read` and `subscribe` deliver; G-11 says how to test an implementation against the wire vectors. `publish` is the home's own fan-out. [ref/wire.py](../../ref/wire.py) implements it. Servers talk to each other (head gossip, notices, blob transfer, `host`) over this binding whatever transport their users have, so a sequencer on the Matrix binding offers it too. Its rules realize T-01…T-05 and T-07:

**G-01 Access follows references; supply follows authors.** A record *references* a blob if its unredacted content holds the `BlobRef` at:
- `content.blobs[]`, for **every** kind, extensions included; or
- `effect.propose.plan.argsBlob`; or
- `effect.outcome.evidence` or `effect.resolve.evidence`.

A **context blob** is the `blob` of a `context` StreamItem (T-07) that a session's actor streamed in `space` (G-05).

`blobGet(ref, space)` succeeds only if both of these hold:
1. **Access:** a record in `space` that the caller may read (P-02) references `ref`; or `ref` is a context blob of a live session of `space` on which `sessionAccess` grants the caller `read` (P-15). Access to a context blob ends when the session is dead or the caller's read right is removed.
2. **Supply:** the server holds the bytes *for that space*. That is true when the referencing entry's author uploaded them to this server (for a context blob: the session's actor), or when the server fetched them for that space under G-02. An upload counts only if **every byte came from that uploader's own `blobPut` calls** and those bytes hash-match. A server MUST NOT report `complete` to an uploader just because the hash is already stored. Storage may be deduplicated by hash, but access never is. Knowing a hash, and mentioning it in a space you control, gets you nothing. That's why a hash proves bytes are equal but never permission, authorship or truth.

Uploaders may read their own unreferenced blobs for 24 h (D3): `blobGet(ref, space)` also succeeds, whether or not any record references `ref`, when the caller's own `blobPut` calls completed `ref` less than 86,400 s earlier on this server's clock and `space` is a space the caller can read at its head (P-02; otherwise `not_found`; a space this server does not hold is answered by §4.1 step 2 first). A server MUST keep a blob while any unredacted record it stores references it, and MAY delete it after.

**`blobPut {ref, offset, data}`** (`data` is base64url, decoded ≤ `maxBlobPartBytes`; D8). The server keeps, per uploader and `ref.hash`, the contiguous bytes it holds from that uploader; `have` is their count. The same actor uploading over two connections continues the same progress.
- Parts are contiguous: `offset` greater than `have` is `invalid` (a gap). A part that overlaps bytes already held MUST carry the same bytes there, else `invalid`, so a resend after a lost reply is harmless.
- `offset + len(data) > ref.size` is `invalid`. Later parts repeat the same `ref.size` and `ref.type` as the first, else `invalid` (`ref.uri` is ignored). A zero-length blob is one part at offset 0 with `data: ""`.
- When `have` reaches `ref.size` the server hashes the bytes. A mismatch is `invalid` and the uploader's bytes for that `ref` are discarded. A match answers `{have: size, complete: true}`; after that any repeat of any part answers `{have: size, complete: true}`.
- `{have, complete: false}` is only ever the answer to a valid partial upload. Malformed base64url, including `=` padding or non-zero trailing bits, is `invalid` (§2). A server MUST bound, per uploader, the bytes it holds in incomplete uploads and the completed bytes no record references yet (the 24 h rule); over the bound `blobPut` is `limit` with `retryAfterMs`.

**`blobGet {ref, space, offset?, length?}`.** A `ref` matches a referenced `BlobRef` by `hash` and `size` (`type` and `uri` are not compared). A caller who fails access or supply (rules 1 and 2 above, or the own-upload rule) gets `not_found`: the answer never confirms that a blob exists. `space` must be a well-formed SpaceId, else `invalid`; a space this server doesn't hold is answered by §4.1 step 2. `offset` defaults to 0 and greater than `ref.size` is `invalid`; `offset = ref.size` answers `data: ""`. `length` defaults to `maxBlobRangeBytes`, is clamped to it (and to the end of the blob), and `length: 0` is `invalid`. The result is `{data, size, offset}`: `offset` is the offset served, `size` the whole blob. Access is evaluated on every call, so a reader of a context blob loses it mid-download when the session dies. If the bytes are missing though supply is claimed, the answer is `unavailable`.

**G-02 Blob transfer.** A client uploads to its own server (`blobPut` as itself; the server may apply quotas). A server fetches lazily: when a reader's `blobGet` finds no bytes held for that space and a record it may read references the blob, the server calls `blobGet` as `@:server` in the context of the referencing space. The server asked answers by G-01 for the server actor: the supply rule holds there when the referencing entry's **author** uploaded the bytes to it (the caller doesn't matter), so the author's server can serve the home. It asks the author's server first, then the home, then any mirror. G-01 is evaluated for the server actor (P-02 server reads). The receiver verifies the hash, moves on to the next server on a mismatch, and from then on holds the bytes for that space.

**G-03 Notices (first contact).** After ordering a `member.set` that gives an actor on server `S` a role, or an entry whose `to` names an actor that holds a role, the home MUST send `signal(profile:<actor>, {type: "notice", space, seq})` to `S` (the roles that trigger it are `guest`, `member` and `owner` set by `member.set`; `banned` and `none` do not; the `to` of a `message` and of a `task` both count). The home sends it over its own `@:home` connection to `S` (G-08). Delivery is best effort: the home retries for 10 minutes and then drops it, duplicates are harmless, and `S` answers `not_found` if it does not host the actor. `S` delivers it **only to that actor's own authenticated sessions**, as signal items on the actor's follow of its own profile space, and never to the profile space's other readers. A notice is only a hint: `S` verifies it by reading the space as `@:S` (P-02 server reads), and drops notices it can't verify. This is how a new member, human or agent, learns that a space exists while it is connected. **A notice is a signal, so it is never stored and never replayed** (G-09): a notice for an actor with no live subscription to its own profile is dropped, and a client that subscribes later is not told what it missed. An actor that was offline learns of its spaces from what it already holds (the space ids it follows, an invitation outside HAN), not from a backlog of notices.

**G-04 Who may signal.** Only actors who may read a space at its head (P-02) may `signal` it (`denied` otherwise) or `subscribe` to it for signals. A `signal` into a space that does not exist is `not_found` (or `moved`, §4.1 step 2). **Across servers:** a user's server relays the user's signal to the home over its own `@:server` connection. The home accepts a relayed `from` only for actors on that server. The home sends signals to other servers' follow streams, and each server fans them out to its local followers. **Profile spaces** are readable by anyone, but only the actor itself may follow its profile for signals, and the only signals they accept are G-03 notices from server actors. The move handshake (§7.3) is therefore not carried on profile spaces: its signals travel in the moved space, among readers (the owners, and the home as `@:A`), and its server-to-server part is the binding call `host`, from the home to the target, authenticated as `@:A`. On the wire a relayed signal carries `from` (the user it speaks for) beside the space and body; a client's own signal omits it, and the connection's authenticated actor is the sender. A subscriber who may read a profile space but is not its actor receives its records only. Servers rate-limit signals per actor (`limit`) and drop them under load; a signal body is Json (I-JSON) of at most 64 KiB of JCS. Signals go to every subscription that may read the space at its head, except those on the sender's own connection. The relayed `from` is accepted only on a connection authenticated as a server actor `@:S` and only for an ActorId on `S` (a `from` naming an actor of another server is `denied`; a `from` that is itself a server actor `@:S` is `invalid`, since only users are relayed); on any other connection `from` is `invalid` (D10). A relayed signal is judged by the read right of `from` at the head, not of the relaying `@:S`, and the per-actor signal limit counts it against `from`; the values of the limits are the server's. Items delivered to subscribers carry `from` = the relayed actor, or the connection's actor when `from` is absent.

**G-05 Session streams.** `stream(item)` is accepted only from the session's actor, on a live session, with `n` one more than its last item (a repeat of the last item is ok). `watch(space, session, as)` delivers the session's `StreamItem`s (han.ts) to `as` while `sessionAccess` grants it watch, and items of type `context` while it grants read.

  *`stream {item}`*: the checks run in this order and the first failure answers. Not authenticated: `denied`. The item is not a closed `StreamItem` of han.ts (grammar, `time` in the Time grammar with no skew check, JCS ≤ `maxStreamItemBytes`): `invalid`. The space is not held here: §4.1 step 2. No session with this id in `space` (unknown, or closed: a closed session has left the state, F3 b): `not_found`. The caller is not the session's actor, or the session is dead by P-15: `denied`. `n` counts per session from 1; `n` equal to the last accepted item's `n` is ok (`ok: null`, not delivered again) only if the item is JCS-identical to it, else `invalid`; `n` that is not last+1 is `invalid`. An accepted item is delivered to every `watch` of the session whose actor has the right, the session's own actor included.
  *`watch {space, session}`*: exactly those two members. Unknown space: §4.1 step 2. Unknown or closed session: `not_found`. A session that is dead, or a caller without the watch right: `denied`. There is no initial reply; only items published after the call are delivered (no replay), as `{id, item: StreamItem}`; `context` items only if the caller also has read. If the right is later removed, or the session closes, the call stays open and delivers nothing more (it resumes if the right returns); the watcher learns of the closure from the log, and ends the call with `cancel`. The head used for access at delivery is the head the home holds when it handles the `stream` call. The home evaluates access at the head when it delivers each item, so nothing published after a receipt that removed a right reaches the actor that lost it. The blob of a `context` item is then readable by `blobGet(ref, space)` for every actor who has `read` on that live session (G-01); the supply is the session's actor's own upload.

**G-06 Move handover.** `host {space, records, head?}` (han.ts `BindingCalls`) is accepted only on a connection authenticated as a server actor `@:A` that is the home of `space` at the last record the target holds (R-02), and only with records contiguous from the target's `have + 1`. The target verifies them (R-14), answers `{have, accept?}` (§7.3), and is not yet a home: it serves no reads of the space and orders nothing until it holds a `space.move` to itself. A `host` call that can't be verified is `invalid`; one the target won't take is `denied`; a `host` from a connection that is not authenticated as a server actor is `denied`; rate limits apply (`limit`). The checks run in this order and the first failure answers: authenticated as a server actor (`denied`, before the args are looked at); the args (`invalid`); `@:A` is the home at the last record B holds, and B is willing (`denied`); then the records, contiguity and R-14 (`invalid`). **B verifies everything**, the entry signatures against the pinned profile views and the redaction proofs included, and never skips a check it cannot make: if a pinned profile cannot be had now (a remote server is unreachable) B answers `unavailable` with `retryAfterMs` and keeps only the records it has verified. **`have`** is what B holds durably, and A continues from `have + 1` whatever it was told before; a B that keeps a partial chain only in memory answers `have: 0` after a restart and A sends the chain again. **`head`**: B answers `accept` only when, after this call, it holds exactly the chain through `head.seq` and the receipt of that record is `head`; if it holds less it answers `{have}` alone, and if `head` is not the receipt of its record at that seq the call is `invalid`.

**G-07 Connection and frames.** *Endpoint.* The server listens for WebSocket at the path `/han` (`wss://<server>/han`; `ws://` for loopback and tests) and MUST upgrade it; it MAY upgrade other paths. No subprotocol is negotiated and `Origin` is not checked. A client dials the `sequencer` URL of `/.well-known/han`, or `wss://<server>/han` when it has none, never a bare host (D7). *Messages* are WebSocket text messages, each one I-JSON object in UTF-8, at most `maxFrameBytes` (1,310,720) of UTF-8; a longer message, or a binary message, closes the connection (codes 1009 and 1003). The server speaks first with the `Challenge` (G-08).
*Request frame* (D2) is exactly `{id, call, args}`: `id` an integer in [1, 2^53), `call` one of the call names of §3 and §8.3, `args` an object (present even when empty) holding only the members documented for that call (every `args` is closed). Replies are `{id, ok}`, `{id, error: WireError}`, `{id, item}` and `{id, end: true}`; a WireError holds `code`, `message` (free text, never normative) and the members `retryAfterMs` and `movedTo` where §3 says.
*What is answered, and with which id.* (1) Text that is not an I-JSON object (unparseable, a duplicate key, a fractional number or exponent, `-0`, an array, a scalar, `null`): `{id: 0, error: invalid}`, and the server closes the connection. A client that receives an error with `id: 0` MUST treat every request still open on the connection as failed. (1a) Text that is an I-JSON object but holds an integer outside [−(2^53−1), 2^53−1], that is 2^53 or more or below −(2^53−1), anywhere (as a number, in `args`, in an entry, in `id`; a negative one is treated exactly like a positive one of the same size): `{id: <the top-level `id` when it is an integer in [1, 2^53)>, error: invalid}` and the connection stays open; when there is no such usable `id`, `{id: 0, error: invalid}` and the server closes the connection, as in (1). A negative integer inside that range is not (1a): it is an ordinary number, which every position that takes a Json number or a count refuses by section 2 (`invalid` on the frame's own id, or id 0 and no close when the frame is not a request frame, as in (2)). (2) A valid I-JSON object that is not a request frame (a member missing or extra, `args` absent or not an object, `call` not a string or unknown, `id` not in [1, 2^53)): `{id: <its id when that is usable, else 0>, error: invalid}`; the connection stays open. (3) A request frame: before a successful `hello` every call except `hello` is `denied`; then args are checked (`invalid`), then the call runs. The order is (1), (1a), (2), authentication, args.
*Order.* A server processes the frames of one connection in arrival order, so `append`s from one connection reach the log in the order sent (R-07), and replies to non-streaming calls come in request order. A request id may be reused once its reply has been sent. A streaming call (`read`, `subscribe`, `watch`) whose id is still open is `invalid` (the error carries that id and the open stream is unaffected). `cancel {target}` ends the open streaming call with request id `target` silently (no `end`, no further frame for it) and answers `ok: null`; a `target` that is unknown or already ended also answers `ok: null`. Per connection, at most `maxFollows` open calls among `read` with `follow`, `subscribe` and `watch`; one more is `limit`. A subscriber that falls too far behind gets `{id, error: unavailable}` and the stream ends.

**G-08 `hello`.** *Challenge.* The first message, `{challenge, server, versions: [1], time}`, is bare (no `id`). `challenge` is 32 bytes from a cryptographic random source written as base64url (43 characters, canonical); a client accepts 22–128 characters of `[A-Za-z0-9_-]`. It is fresh per connection, single-use (consumed by the first `hello` with well-formed args, successful or not; one that is `invalid` for its args or `version` leaves it unconsumed) and valid for 60 s. A server MUST bound the connections, and so the challenges, it issues per client address; over the bound it refuses the upgrade with HTTP 429 and a `Retry-After` header instead of issuing another. `server` is the ServerName the server believes it is; `time` its clock.
*Proof.* `hello {who, keyId, proof, version: 1, knownHeads?}` (exactly these members). `proof` is the 64-byte Ed25519 signature, base64url without padding (§2), over the bytes

```
"han-hello-v1\n"  ‖  UTF-8(challenge, as sent: the 43 characters, not a decoding of them)  ‖  "\n"  ‖  UTF-8(dialed)
```

`dialed` is the ServerName the client intends, from the space id, the actor id or the `.well-known` entry it followed, **never** the host or port it connected to. A client MUST abort unless the challenge's `server` equals `dialed`. The server verifies with its own name: a proof over any other name or any other challenge, a challenge that is used or expired, a bad signature, a `keyId` that is absent from the profile, revoked or marked unusable: `denied`. Worked bytes: conformance/wire-vectors/README.md.
*Codes* (D11). `version` other than 1 or malformed args: `invalid`. `who` is an actor this server cannot authenticate (no profile space here): `not_found`; a server actor `@:S'` of another server is authenticated with the keys in S''s `/.well-known/han`. A second `hello` on an authenticated connection: `invalid` (the connection stays authenticated). A failed `hello` leaves the connection open and unauthenticated; every call but `hello` is `denied`, and a later `hello` on it is `denied` too (the challenge is used, unless the failure was `invalid`): the client opens a new connection.
*Result.* `{limits, durability, time, knownHeads}`. `limits` has exactly the <!--s:limits_w-->ten<!--/s--> members of han.ts `Limits` (D9), all integers; a client ignores members it does not know and fails the connection if one is missing.

**G-09 Reading, state and subscribing.** `read` takes exactly `{space, after, follow?, limit?, knownHeads?}` (an unknown member, `state`, `at`, `cursor` and `effect` included, is `invalid`); `after` is a Seq ≥ 0. Items come in this order and no other: `heads` (if `knownHeads` was sent, possibly with an empty list), then `record`s in seq order, only for seqs the reader may read (P-02). `after` at or beyond the head gives no record, and without `follow` the stream then ends. `read` with `follow` continues with new records, only for seqs the reader may read when they are published; **it never carries signals**. `state` takes exactly `{space}` plus at most one of `at`, `cursor`, `effect`, `agent`, except that `at` may join `agent` (an unknown member is `invalid`; any other two selectors are `invalid` by presence, judged before any value is read) and answers `{id, ok: {state, next?}}`, one frame within `maxFrameBytes` (§3), with no stream and no `end`.
`subscribe {space, after?}` (exactly these members) delivers **records and signals**: without `after` it is live only; with `after` it replays the records after `after` atomically with registration (no gap), then continues; it does not end. A reader who may not read the space at its head gets `denied`, except on a profile space, which anyone may subscribe to and then receives records only (G-04). A space the server doesn't hold is answered by §4.1 step 2, at once; a client that expects the space retries (§3, D5). Signals are never replayed, notices (G-03) included.

**G-10 Server-to-server.** Servers dial each other over this binding, authenticated as `@:S` (G-08), for notices (G-03), relayed signals (G-04), blob transfer (G-02), profile reads (§4.1 step 4) and `host` (G-06). The `/.well-known/han` and `/.well-known/han-peers` documents (§4.3) are served over HTTP(S) on the same listener.

**G-11 Wire conformance (D15, D16; decisions in [spec/decisions/DECISIONS-r21.md](../decisions/DECISIONS-r21.md)).** [conformance/wire-vectors/](../../conformance/wire-vectors/) holds one JSON file per operation, taken from the transcripts both reference servers answered byte for byte alike, plus the agreed error paths, and `python ref/run_wire_vectors.py --url ws://host:port/han` replays them against any server. A server passes when every `expect` frame matches after the masking documented in [conformance/wire-vectors/README.md](../../conformance/wire-vectors/README.md). A server under test runs in **test mode**: the ServerName `a.localhost`; a test clock (wall time `2026-01-01T00:00:00Z` and steady time 0 at the start; neither moves by itself, only `test.clock` moves them); the server key of `conformance/wire-vectors/config.json` with `keyId` `h1` and `from` `1970-01-01T00:00:00Z`; the profile spaces of the listed actors provisioned (`@alice`, `@bob`, `@carol`, `@vera`: `human`, `@x`: `service`, each with key `k1`); an empty data directory. How it is put into this mode (flags) is the implementation's; the repository's `conformance/interop/py_host.py` and `conformance/interop/ts_host.mjs` are two examples.

**`test.limits {maxFrameBytes?}`** is the second control call of test mode (han.ts `TestCalls`), added so that the byte cut of a state page (§3, H3) can be tested between two implementations without a 1.25 MiB state. `maxFrameBytes` is an integer from 1,024 to 1,310,720 (anything else, a boolean and any other member are `invalid`; before `hello` it is `denied`). From then on `hello` reports that `maxFrameBytes`, and state pages are cut to it by the rule of §3. The size limit of an incoming message (G-07) is not lowered, so a vector may still send frames up to the real limit. The answer is `ok: {maxFrameBytes}`, the value in force (`{}` only reads it). A vector that lowers it puts it back to 1,310,720 before it ends, and keeps its states small enough that the repeated members and any one item fit the lowered value (the server asserts, §3). Outside test mode it is not a call, like `test.clock`.

**`test.clock {wall?, steadyAdvance?}`** is a control call of test mode, and of nothing else (han.ts `TestCalls`). It is an ordinary request frame on an authenticated connection (before `hello` it is `denied`, like every call). `wall` (a Time) sets the wall reading to that instant, forward or back; `steadyAdvance` (an integer ≥ 0, in seconds) advances the steady reading, which never goes back; both, one or neither (`{}` only reads) may be given. The answer is `ok: {wall, steady}`, the readings after the call (`steady` in whole seconds). From then on every receipt time, every `time` of a Challenge and of a `hello` answer, and every wait that R-16 and R-03a measure, comes from those two readings: in test mode the receipt time is the wall reading itself, and the A of R-16 is counted on the steady one, so the two can be moved separately (a wall step does not shorten an abandon wait; a steady advance without a wall step does not make a receipt later). A `wall` that is not a Time, a `steadyAdvance` that is negative or not an integer, and any other member are `invalid`. **Outside test mode `test.clock` is not a call**: a server answers `invalid`, as for any unknown call (G-07 (2)), and a server meant for production MUST NOT offer it. The test hosts add it in process (`conformance/interop/py_host.py`, `conformance/interop/ts_host.mjs`), and `test.limits` likewise; the stock servers have neither. The runner reaches them through a `clock` step and a `limits` step (conformance/wire-vectors/README.md), and a vector that moves the wall clock puts it back before it ends.

### 8.4 Profile: Claude Code as a session (informative)

How a harness maps onto P-15 and R-24, with Claude Code as the example. The *host* is a small process holding the agent's HAN key, which runs Claude Code through the Claude Agent SDK in streaming-input mode, so it controls every turn.
- **Open.** The host appends `session.open {steerMode}` as the agent and publishes `output` items from the SDK's message stream and `status` items from its turn state (and from OSC 7501 program-status reports where the harness emits them).
- **Context.** On request, or periodically, it publishes a `context` item: transcript and plan as an encrypted blob.
- **Steer in.** A `session.input` with a receipt becomes a user turn. For a turn R-24 makes strict, the host implements the stricter mode: it sets the SDK permission mode to `default` (instead of the session's `acceptEdits` or `bypassPermissions`) with no allow rules, and its tool gate turns every call that reaches the world into an effect (below), never approving one itself. The host MAY first show the input to the agent's human. Per R-24, the stricter mode is used for every turn driven by someone else's instruction (steer, task, effect naming it) while the agent is guarded; for every turn driven by a guarded author or by a task with a `guardedFor`, whatever the agent's guarding; and for every turn, once the agent is guarded, of an agent that has taken an outside instruction in any session.
- **Seeing every tool call.** `canUseTool` is called only for calls the CLI itself would ask about: reads inside the working directory, and edits there in `acceptEdits`, never reach it. A gate built on `canUseTool` alone therefore can't veto them. The host MUST see every tool call with a `PreToolUse` hook (or an equivalent that fires for all calls) and decide there, and MAY keep `canUseTool` as a second check. Allowing everything inside the working directory is then the gate's decision, not a side effect of the CLI's rules.
- **World actions.** The host starts the SDK with `settingSources: []` (so no project or user settings add allow rules) and gives it **no allow rules** in a strict turn. In a strict turn, a call that would reach the world becomes `effect.propose`, and the model is answered at once, as a denial of that call carrying the text "proposed as <id>, waiting for <guard>" (the only way to answer a call without running it; no blocking, so no hook timeout). The id is real: the host signs the proposal when it answers, and appends it once the turn is journaled. A refusal (no executor, over budget) is answered "refused: …". `WebFetch` and `WebSearch` are world reads that can leak context through URLs, so they are proposed as effects too; MCP servers other than HAN's own are disabled for those turns. While guarded, file edits (`Write`, `Edit`, `NotebookEdit`) are refused like Bash writes and the model is pointed to the proposal path (H11); in `auto` turns edits inside the session's workspace stay local. Pushes, deploys, payments and outside messages reach the world only through an executor, as effects. The host, outside the session, holds the agent's HAN key and Matrix credentials and talks to the homeserver, sequencer and executor; nothing inside the session can post to any room. In a budgeted space the host's proposals carry `cost` and name the agent's assigned budgeted task (P-05, P-06), or Policy denies them. Dependencies are fetched by the host before the turn (or as effects), since even a read-only mirror's request URLs can leak context.
- **Sandbox.** Where an OS sandbox exists (the SDK's `sandbox` is macOS and Linux only), the session runs in it with network egress limited to the model API and the host, and it must cover every process the session can start, not only the shell tool. **Without one (Windows), `guarded` is advisory**: a shell can do what the gate's patterns miss. The host MUST then say so in its `status` items, for example `status.detail = "guarded: advisory (no sandbox)"`, and a client SHOULD show it.
- **Read others.** An MCP tool lets the agent `watch` sessions it has the read right on. The host runs turns that used it as if guarded (R-24).

## 11. Decisions and trade-offs

| Decision | Chosen | Rejected | Cost accepted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scope | the agent-safety layer, over existing transports (Matrix first) | a full chat and federation network | HAN depends on a transport for delivery, identity mapping, files and invites |
| Ordering | one sequencer per space; hash-chained receipts; cooperative moves with signed acceptance | consensus, merged event graphs, forced takeover | no HAN appends while the sequencer is down; after it dies, work continues in a fork |
| Interface | <!--s:calls_core-->4<!--/s--> sequencer calls + typed entries | one RPC per feature (v0.4) | strict schemas carry the detail (R-09) |
| Authorization | pure functions over the log + pinned profiles | live policy service; transport power levels | no outside data in decisions |
| Effects | claim in the same log; launch at once or never; window pinned and stated in the claim; elapsed-time deadlines | attempt/lease registry; clock comparisons | a crash between claim and launch costs one retry |
| Accountability | humans own spaces; agents have confirmed human operators, re-checked at every action | agents as ordinary users | an agent can't administer a space |
| Budget | one budget, one unit, per space | multi-currency budgets | a second currency needs a second space |
| Redaction | permanent header, redactable content, signed proof | full deletion | header fields (who, when, which action) are never erasable |
| Guarded steering | guarding is a fact about the work: who was guarded is recorded in `guardedFor` when a task or effect is created and never shrinks; a claim needs the guard of each of those agents and of a proposer or executor guarded now; a guarded agent may delegate, propose and work, but may not approve, govern or steer others; only its operator ends its choice (`agent.unguard`); owners may force every non-human with `forceGuarded`, decided per entry from the pinned profile; nobody stands in; an approval by a guarded author never counts, for a rule or the cap, and a guard's never counts toward the cap; an assignee carries the guarding of its open tasks into all its proposals | holds and releases (rounds 10–26: five interacting facts, and a new hole in each round); per-turn tracking (impossible: Policy can't see what a model read); taint propagation (leaked in rounds 9–10); owner stand-ins (let an owner approve its own steer); a guard stored in the session or the effect (the pinned profile already names it); a service exemption from forcing (`actorKind` is self-declared: one more label to trust, H6) | every effect a guarded agent proposes or executes, or a delegate proposes while assigned its task, waits for a human, so a delegate needs that human for all its effects until the task closes, and at most 19 guarded agents may stand behind one proposal; in a forced space an effect's service executor is guarded too, so its operator must hold a role there and approve; a subtask without a parent, and work after the delegate closes the task, are not carried (R-24 binds agents' hosts; nothing binds a human); outside instructions are never refused, so a guarded agent's host must run them in its stricter mode; if its operator leaves for good, nothing recorded for a guarded agent can run in that space (owners can cancel it); an agent that chose `guarded` goes back to `auto` only with its operator's signature |
