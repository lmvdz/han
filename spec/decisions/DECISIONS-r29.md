## Round 29 notes

- **T1: the order of `guardedFor`.** An effect's list is the named task's `guardedFor`, then the `guardedFor` of every other task that is `open` at the proposal and lists the proposer in `to`, in the order the tasks were created (their `seq`), then the proposer and the executor if guarded, each agent once. The order matters because the claim's `guardApprovals` follow it, so two implementations must agree on it (vector `guarded-tasks-are-bounded` pins it). The rule is one helper in policy.py (`effect_guarded_for`), used by `decide` and `fold`. No new state field and no wire change: the tasks' `guardedFor`, `to`, `open` and `seq` are already in the state, and every agent in them is already pinned where it matters (P-01 pins an effect's `guardedFor` for an approve, cancel or claim).
- **T1: "open".** "Open" is the task's `open` flag. A `task.cancel` only requests cancellation, so a task with a cancel request still carries its guarding until an assignee (or an owner, force-closing) closes it (vector `assignees-carry-their-tasks-guarding`). Only the proposer's tasks count, as the decision says, not the executor's: a guarded executor is recorded as itself. A task's own `guardedFor` is unchanged (its parent's plus its author if guarded), so a delegate's parentless subtask, and the work of whoever it is assigned to, still carry nothing. That is the residue §7.5 now lists, together with a delegate closing the task itself (a signed `task.update`) and then working on, which the decision accepts ("the effect after the task closes is free").
- **T1: the bound.** It is the last check of `effect.propose` in P-16 (`denied`, reason "too many guarded tasks; close some"), so every other refusal of a proposal comes first. `MAX_GUARDED_FOR` stays 19 in han.ts and policy.py; for an effect it is now checked, for a task it is still derived (a chain has at most 17 authors). The cost is stated in P-15 and §11: a delegate needs the delegator's guard for all its effects until the task closes.
- **T1: R-24.** Naming the driving task stays a host MUST, described as precision (the effect is charged to the task, and a subtask's `parent` is the only link Policy has for it). §7.5's delegation row shrinks to the parentless subtask and the self-closed task; the README, goal.md and §11 say the same.
- **T2: forcing is judged with pinned profiles, so they are pinned.** For an `effect.approve` while `forceGuarded` is on, P-01 (and `named_actors`, so a missing profile is `invalid`, P-16 step 2) adds the authors of the effect's unconsumed approvals, at most 36. Without forcing no profile is needed: the fold already drops the approvals of authors that lost their role or chose guarding, so every remaining one by a non-guard is countable. Receipts of approvals in a forced space pin more profiles; no golden transcript has one (make_wire_vectors reports no STALE GOLDEN). This is a pin change, not a state or wire change.
- **T2: an approval's fold drops forced approvals.** The decision drops approvals on a lost role and on joining `guarded`; it says nothing of forced authors, whose approvals the cap no longer counts. Kept in the state, they would break the derived bounds (16 agents approve, forcing goes on, 16 humans approve: 32 approvals by authors that are never guards, against the 17 that `MAX_UNCONSUMED_APPROVALS` = 36 assumes). So the fold of every `effect.approve` also drops the open approvals whose authors are guarded by the profiles pinned for it, which are exactly the forced ones (the chosen ones are gone already). A `policy.set` that switches forcing on can't drop them (its fold has no profile of theirs), so until the next approval on that effect, switching forcing off makes them count again (vector `cap-counts-only-countable-approvals`). With this the bounds, and han.ts' `MAX_UNCONSUMED_APPROVALS` (36) and `MAX_APPROVALS` (55), are unchanged, and fuzz_fold asserts them as before.
- **T2: returned approvals.** A not-started outcome returns its consumed approvals; one whose author has meanwhile lost its role or chosen guarding is dropped then. So the fold keeps an invariant the guard model checks (DeadApprovalsDropped) and the fuzzer asserts: no effect holds an unconsumed approval by an author without a role or in `guarded`. The cap's "holds a role" test in `decide` is kept as the decision words it, though the invariant makes it redundant.
- **T2: supersede exists.** The fold of an `effect.approve` already drops its author's open approvals on that proposal (P-08), so an approver whose key was revoked approves again with a valid key, and its old approval stops counting (vector `revoked-keys-still-count-until-superseded`). No new kind. Key revocation stays invisible to the fold; P-08 and §7.5 say so.
- **T2: a consequence for guards.** A guard that loses its role loses its open approvals, so E3's "while the guard holds a role" can no longer fail on an approval that exists. The check stays in the code; the mutation that removed it (model_guard `guard-approval-needs-no-role`, mutate_vectors "guard approval needs no role") is now equivalent and is retired from both suites. `departed-guard` still holds as written.
- **T2: the liveness property (T9).** model_guard checks CapLiveness: an approval by an unguarded role holder that is none of the effect's guards is never refused for the cap while fewer than the cap of countable approvals exist, counted by the ghost (role, its own record of who is guarded, the guards it requires). Its four approvers can't reach 16, so the model lowers `MAX_OPEN_APPROVALS` to 1 (with 2, the first approval made while BOT2 is forced drops BOT2's before a second could be refused, and the forced-count mutants slip through). The ghost also drops its record of an approval when the author loses its role or chooses guarding, and it checks DeadApprovalsDropped.
- **T7.** Only the approval clause of the cancel right is affected; the proposer's, a guard's and an owner's rights are not. "Guarded at the cancel's seq" is `guardStatus` with the profile pinned for the cancel (the author is always pinned), so a forced approver regains the right if forcing goes off, unless an approval made while it was forced dropped its approval (vector `guarded-approvals-grant-no-cancel`). A chosen guarding drops the approval at once, so in practice the check matters for forcing.
- **T8.** A non-human profile without `operator` is `invalid` at P-16 common step 4 (with the other profile-space checks); a human profile may name none. han.ts declares `operator?: ActorId | null` in both `ProfileView` and the profile state, with the rule in the comments (an ActorId for an agent or service; absent or null only for a human). A type-level union on `actorKind` was rejected to keep the declarations simple and the consistency check unchanged. No vector or wire scenario had an agent profile without an operator.
- **T4.** R-24 gets a normative bullet, **The stricter mode**, for every agent, guarded or not: no allow rules; every call that reaches outside the sandbox becomes an `effect.propose` naming the driving task, if any; nothing approved locally; who approves is Policy's business (E3). §8.4 now presents Claude Code's permission mode `default` plus the gate as one way to implement it, and its "while guarded" wording became "in a strict turn".
- **T5.** The taint is per agent. "Once it is guarded" is read per space: a later turn runs strict once the agent is guarded in that turn's space, since guarding is per space. §7.5's cross-space row now says the taint crosses spaces, instead of "not carrying an outside steer across spaces". The host tests cited in §10 are round 28's; the row says the round-29 duties (T4, T5) are not tested yet.
