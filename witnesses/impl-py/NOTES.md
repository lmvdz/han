# Implementation notes: spec gaps and the readings taken

Each entry says where the spec is silent or ambiguous, what `witnesses/impl-py/` does (always the most conservative reading), and the section it touches. Nothing in `ref/` was changed.

## Wire framing (§3, §8.3, han.ts)

1. **Frames for the six transport operations are not in han.ts** (`RequestFrame` covers only `hello`/`append`/`read`). Defined here, same envelope `{id, call, args}`: `subscribe {space, after?}`, `signal {space, body}`, `blobPut {ref, offset, data(base64url)}` → `{have, complete}`, `blobGet {ref, space, offset?, length?}` → `{data, size, offset}` (at most `maxBlobRangeBytes` per call), `stream {item}`, `watch {space, session}` (replies are `item` frames carrying a `StreamItem`). A `cancel {target}` call ends a streaming call (needed to stop a follow without closing the socket); it is not in §3/§8.3. Proposed addition to han.ts.
2. `hello` must be the first call; the server speaks first with the `Challenge` object (§3, han.ts `Challenge`). Calls before it are `denied`.
3. `subscribe` without `after` is live-only; with `after` it replays first, atomically with registration (no gap). §8.3 says only "subscribe is called follow in ref/wire.py".
4. A connection may append an entry whose `author` is not the connection's actor (R-12: authorship is the signature, never the sender).

## Identity and profiles (§4.1 step 4, §4.3, R-10)

5. **Profiles of actors on other servers (federation, section below).** An actor of another server has a pinnable profile once this server has read, verified and cached its profile space (`han.seq.federation`). Without a `Federation` attached (a bare `Sequencer`), or while a peer cannot be reached, appends needing it answer `unavailable` (§4.1 step 4). The key window `from` of this server's own key is `1970-01-01T00:00:00Z`.
6. **How a profile space is first created is not specified over the wire.** §4.3 only says the server actor appends `space.create` with `profile`. Here the server signs it with its own home key as `@:S` (the synthetic view of `@:S` lists the home key as valid), through the normal pipeline. Trigger: `python -m han.seq register …`, `--register` at startup, or `Sequencer.register()`.
7. The pinned profile seq is the profile space's head at append time (fresh by construction, R-10). For a claim the sequencer pins the approval candidates in E3 order only up to and including the first whose key is valid (`Sequencer.pin_actors`, matching `Home.pin`); an unreachable later approver cannot make the claim `unavailable`, and federation prefetches them one at a time in the same order. Records store the pinned views so a reload replays `fold` without the profile spaces.
8. `hello` accepts `@:S` (this server's actor) with the server key. This is the "home reads its own space" actor (P-02); it equals root on this server, which is what it is.

## Clock and time (R-03a, R-16)

9. **R-03a "refuse with `unavailable` if the clock is behind"**: no tolerance. The receipt clock is `wall at startup + steady elapsed`; if it is earlier than the space's last receipt time, every append that is not a dedupe hit answers `unavailable` with `retryAfterMs` = the gap, until it catches up. After a restart on a clock that is hours behind, the server is unavailable for hours (as written). The "persisted last receipt time" is the chain itself (per space, which is what R-03a requires), not a separate global value.
10. **R-16 error code (now the spec).** The spec says the sequencer "MUST NOT order" an abandon before A steady seconds; it does not say what the sender sees. Answer: `unavailable` with `retryAfterMs` (the same request succeeds later), not `denied`, so a client's R-06 retry delivers it when due. It applies only to an owner's `effect.outcome` with `unknown/unknown` by someone other than the claiming instance (P-09) that Policy would allow once claim.time + A has passed (the claim is the latest, its phase `claimed`); anything else, e.g. an abandon after `succeeded`, gets Policy's verdict (`denied`) at once. A claim ordered in an earlier run counts from the start of this run.
11. The R-16 check runs after dedupe (R-01 first) and before Policy, so an early abandon is `unavailable` instead of Policy's `denied "abandon before the launch window closed"`.

## Reads, follow, limits (§3)

12. `read` with `follow` and a backlog larger than `limit`: the backlog is cut at `limit`, then the live stream continues from records newer than the head at registration. The skipped records are a visible gap in `seq`; `Client.read` re-reads the gap by itself (right after `limit` records, and whenever a record arrives past a gap), so a follower sees every readable record once, in order. A hole nobody may read (P-02) ends the fill.
13. `subscribe`/`read follow` on a `profile:` space delivers its records to anyone (profile spaces are world-readable, §4.3); only the actor itself also gets notices (G-03/G-04 say "only the actor may follow its profile for signals").
14. A reader with no readable seq gets `not_found`; `subscribe` on a space the caller can't read gets `denied` (as wire.Home.follow). These differ on purpose of the two spec/ref texts; both avoid revealing more than the reference.
15. Rate limits (`limit` errors, §3, G-04) and `maxFollows` are not enforced. Header size is measured as the JCS of the whole entry including `sig` (≤ 16384), content as JCS (≤ 65536).

## Blobs, notices, sessions (G-01…G-05)

16. **Upload order.** Supply (G-01) is decided by wire.Home when the referencing record is ordered: the author must have completed the upload before the append. An upload after the append does not retroactively supply. Conservative; clients upload first.
17. **Context blobs of `StreamItem`s.** `blobGet(ref, space)` succeeds for an actor with the `read` right (`session_access`) on a live session of that space whose `context` item references the blob, provided the session's actor uploaded it (supply, G-01). A member who only watches, a closed session, or bytes uploaded by someone else do not qualify. The check is `Sequencer._context_blob` and runs only after the record-based rule (G-01) refused.
18. **Own blobs (G-01, D3).** "Uploaders may read their own unreferenced blobs for 24 h" is implemented: a completed upload is stamped (`uploads.at`) and its uploader may `blobGet` it for 86400 s (`Sequencer._own_blob`) with `space` any space it can read. Uploads are not garbage-collected after that (they simply stop being readable by the uploader alone).
19. Notices (G-03) are only delivered between actors of this server. Since round 23 (F4) they are signals: pushed to the actor's live subscription of its own profile and otherwise dropped, never stored and never replayed (the `notices` table is gone). They are hints and not acknowledged.
20. `stream` re-send: a repeat of the last accepted item (same `n`, identical content) is `ok`, so a client that lost the reply can retry; any other `n` that is not last+1 is `invalid` (G-05 only says "n one more than its last item"). `watch` delivers live items only (no backlog), ends only when the connection or the call is closed, and does not announce a lost right.
21. Items of a `StreamItem` are stored (SQLite) but only `n` is used after a restart.
22. `blobPut` completion is idempotent per uploader: if the uploader already completed that hash, a repeat answers `complete` at once (needed when the last reply is lost). In-progress parts are kept in memory and lost on restart; the client resumes at the `have` offset the server reports.

## State at a past seq

27. `state {space, at: N}` (round 24; before, `read` with `state` and `at`) returns the state after record `N` (folded from the chain with the profile views each receipt pinned) instead of the head state. It needs the read right at `N`, judged on the CURRENT state (P-02: a member who joined at seq 3 reads the state at seq 2; a server denied now reads nothing) (`denied` otherwise), `1 <= N <= head` (`invalid` otherwise). `read` is entries only.

## Verification (R-14, R-15)

23. The server returns its own heads for `knownHeads` (hello and `read`) but does not compute equivocation proofs itself; the asker (a mirror) compares. `Sequencer.profile(actor, seq)` gives a mirror the `profile_source` it needs.
24. Durability is only `"local"` (SQLite WAL, `synchronous=FULL`). A space whose `minDurability` is `replicated` gets `unavailable` (R-08), as wire.Home does.
25. A storage error after wire.Home has already advanced its in-memory state makes the sequencer stop serving (`unavailable`) rather than risk a receipt for a record that is not on disk; restart reloads from disk. The reload re-verifies every stored receipt chain and signature and refuses to start on a mismatch.
26. Moves (`space.move`) need the target server's key, which this server doesn't know, so a move is `invalid` here; a stored move would be honoured on reload (`moved`).

## Federation between sequencers (§4.3, G-02, G-03, G-04, P-01, R-10; `han/seq/federation.py`)

28. **`.well-known` on the WebSocket port.** `GET /.well-known/han` and `/.well-known/han-peers` are answered on the same port as the WebSocket (a plain GET before the upgrade), over HTTP in tests and local setups; TLS is the deployment's business (§4.3 says HTTPS). `han` = `{server, sequencer, keys: [{keyId, publicKey, from, until?, compromised?}]}`: `sequencer` is `--advertise` or the full URL `ws://HOST:PORT/han` (D7, D13). `han-peers` = `{peers: [{server, url}]}`: the other servers whose keys this one verifies with (never itself), `url` where it finds them (the peers map, else `https://<server>`).
29. **Two things are called han-peers.** The spec's `/.well-known/han-peers` is a served document of retained key sets; the config asked for here (known peers, name → URL) is a local file, `--peers han-peers.json` / `--peer NAME=URL`: `{"b.localhost": "http://127.0.0.1:8702"}`, the base URL of that server's `/.well-known/han`. A server not listed is looked up at `https://<name>`. The spec should give the config a different name.
30. **Remote actors dial the home.** A remote actor `hello`s to the home with the home's name in the proof (§3) and appends there; nothing in the spec says whether it may instead go through its own server. Here its own server only relays signals (G-04) and serves its profile; appends, reads and follows of a space go to the home. The profile is read by the home as `@:home` over the generic binding from the actor's server and checked with `wire.Mirror` (receipt signature and key window under the peer's keys, chain, entry signatures, Policy replay), then kept in SQLite (`peer_records`) and served again by `read` of that profile space (verifiers serve what they pinned, §4.3). The spec does not say how a profile space is verified; the only rule here is the same as a space's.
31. **Freshness (R-10) is measured on the home's steady clock** from the start of the read: a cached head view is used for 300 s, then read again before the next append or hello that needs it. Reading earlier is always allowed, and happens when the entry's key is not valid in the cached view (a new device) so a `key.add` is seen at once. A stale view is never used: a peer that cannot be reached makes the append `unavailable`. After a restart every view counts as stale. A record the home has already ordered keeps the profile seq it pinned (`_state_at` folds from the kept chain).
32. **Pinned profiles of third servers.** A profile record may itself pin actors of a third server (an operator's confirmation in an agent's profile space on b, with the operator on a, is pinned by b's receipt). Their chains are fetched first; a cycle is cut (that view is then missing and the profile is `unavailable`). The spec doesn't say how deep to follow.
33. **Server keys.** Peer keys are fetched when a profile or hello needs them and the copy is 30 s old (`KEYS_TTL`; a view older than 300 s is not used). A key that changes for a seen window (public key, `from`, a set `until`, a cleared `compromised`, or a key that disappears) is a fault: the peer is distrusted until restart (not persisted). `compromised`: wire.verify_receipt only knows windows, so the home ends the key's window just after the latest receipt of it that it had recorded when it first saw the mark (or at `from`: nothing). Receipts it recorded before still verify; nothing new under that key does, and `@:S` cannot `hello` with it. A profile record of an unseen actor signed by that key after the cut is `unavailable`, even if it was genuine. "Recorded before first seeing the mark" is read as the receipts in this server's kept profile chains (the spec says "a verifier recorded"). A server's own rotation (`Sequencer.rotate_key`, `mark_compromised`) is API only, no CLI.
34. **Notices across servers (G-03).** After ordering a `member.set` (or a `to`) that names an actor on another server, the home sends `signal(profile:<actor>, {type: "notice", space, seq})` over its `@:home` connection, three tries over about 2 s, then gives up (hints). The receiving server accepts one only from the home of the named space, for its own actor, and drops it unless it reads that space as `@:S` and finds the actor holding a role and `head >= seq`; the rest is the local mechanism (G-03 inbox, delivered to the actor's own follow of its profile).
35. **Blob transfer (G-02).** The home orders first, then fetches (a record referencing a blob is ordered before the bytes are here; supply is separate, G-01). It asks the author's server and then the home, with `blobGet` as `@:me`; the client checks the hash; the bytes are held for that space (`supplied`), never as an upload by the author. A reader's `blobGet` waits (at most 15 s) for a fetch under way, then answers as before (`unavailable` if still not supplied). The serving server has no copy of the space, so G-01's "access" is evaluated by reading the space from its home as `@:me` and applying `blob_readable` to the requesting server actor (P-02 server read); it serves if the author of such a record uploaded the bytes there, or it fetched them for that space. The spec says "G-01 is evaluated for the server actor" but not where the records come from. Not done: mirrors in the fetch order (no mirror list exists); a user of server B reading a blob of a space homed on A through B.
36. **Signal relay (G-04).** A local user's `signal` for a space homed elsewhere is relayed over the server connection with an added `from` field (not in han.ts `signal`); the home accepts `from` only from `@:S` for an actor on S, and checks the read right of that actor. Not done: the other direction in full (the home does deliver signals to a server actor that follows, since `@:S` can read; but a server does not yet follow foreign spaces for its local followers and fan them out), per-actor rate limits.
37. **Server connections and kinds of frames.** A server connects with `hello` as `@:me` using its own key. The `Client` class takes a loaded `key` for this. Server actors can read any space they may (P-02) and can relay signals; they are not otherwise restricted from `append` (authorship is the signature, R-12).
